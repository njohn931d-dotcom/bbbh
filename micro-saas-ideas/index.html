23 Micro-SaaS Ideas Worth Building in 2026 (+ Validation Playbook)

The indie pattern that keeps working in 2026: one founder, one narrow professional pain, $19–$99 per month. Here are 23 pains worth solving, and the gate we run before building any of them.

The micro-SaaS pattern that keeps working in 2026 is deliberately small: one founder, one narrow professional pain, $19–99 per month. At $49/month you need 20 customers for a $9,800/month run-rate — a number one person can reach through search content and one community, without venture capital or a team. Below are 23 pains we would build against today, each with the buyer, the price point and the fastest MVP path, followed by the five-question gate we run before writing a line of code. For the wider picture, see the 21-method income guide.

What makes these “worth building”

Every idea below passes four filters: the buyer is a professional who already pays for tools; the problem is repetitive (weekly or daily); the buyer is reachable in one channel; and an MVP is weekend-scale. Anything failing a filter goes in the bin, not the backlog.

The five-question validation gate

Before any code, every idea above — including your own — must pass all five. Two “no” answers and it is a hobby, not a business.

  1. Frequency: does the buyer hit this problem weekly or more? Monthly problems don’t sustain subscriptions; they sustain one-off tools.
  2. Budget: does this buyer already pay for adjacent software? “People who pay for nothing” is not a market, it’s an audience.
  3. Reach: can you name the one channel where these buyers gather (a subreddit, a Slack, a trade newsletter, a marketplace)? If you need three channels to find ten customers, stop.
  4. MVP: can the smallest honest version ship in a weekend or two? If not, decompose until it can.
  5. Pre-pay: will three strangers put money down on a description? Pre-orders are the only validation that isn’t politeness.

Running the pre-order test in seven days

  • Day 1–2: landing page with outcome headline, price, and a “founding member — 50% off, 14 days” button that takes a real payment or deposit.
  • Day 2–4: post the build story (not an ad) in your one channel; answer every reply personally.
  • Day 3–6: ten 20-minute conversations with people who clicked; ask about the last time the problem cost them money.
  • Day 7: count pre-orders. ≥3 of 10 conversations converting = build. 0 = refund, rewrite the promise, retest once, then move on.

Three ideas, fully worked

Content-decay detector (#6) — why it wins

Every site with organic traffic has pages silently losing rankings; nobody notices until the quarter is gone. Buyers (content teams, niche-site owners, agencies) already pay for rank trackers but not for the workflow: which page dropped, why, and what the refresh should change. MVP = search-console data in, prioritized refresh queue out. Price $29–99/month scales with tracked pages. Distribution writes itself: publish your own decay reports as public case studies — the marketing is the product demo.

Licence-key & release manager (#13) — the pickaxe seller

Indie developers selling plugins and desktop tools all rebuild the same plumbing: key issuance, validation endpoints, offline grace, changelog pages, renewal reminders. Selling that plumbing is the classic pickaxe play during a gold rush — and RevenueKit’s own SaaS Starter began exactly here. Churn is low because migrating licence infrastructure is painful, which is the moat. Price $19–59/month; expand into release notes hosting and update pings.

Niche dataset API (#23) — boring, defensible, B2B

Curate one dataset nobody else maintains cleanly (we maintain digital-product pricing benchmarks; a reader maintains Shopify-app fee history). Developers pay $29–199/month for metered access because collecting it themselves costs a week per refresh. The work is rigor: documented methodology, versioned snapshots, uptime page. Once trusted, it becomes infrastructure — the stickiest revenue shape on this list.

Where solo founders lose the plot

  • Building platform-first: auth and billing are bought, not built (that’s what boilerplates are for).
  • Pricing at $5: cheap pricing doesn’t reduce churn, it guarantees you can’t afford support.
  • Feature parity ambition: you win on fit for one niche, never on breadth against incumbents.
  • Silent building: no build log, no public numbers → no distribution. Publish weekly from day one.
  • Ignoring annual plans: annual pre-pay fixes cashflow and halves churn in one setting.
