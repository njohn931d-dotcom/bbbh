How Many Hours of Work Goes to Rent? (Tables by Monthly Rent)

See how many hours of work a month your rent costs, what share of take-home pay it consumes, and how to work out the number for your own lease.

A $1,800 monthly rent costs 72 hours of work a month at a $25 take-home hourly rate — $21,600 a year, or about 21.6 forty-hour weeks. Roughly five months of your working year goes to housing before anything else is paid for.

The "30% of income on housing" guideline is widely quoted and widely missed in high-cost cities. It is a benchmark, not a law — and it is measured against gross income in some versions and take-home in others, which changes the answer. What matters more is whether the remaining amount covers everything else you need.

Rent is not the whole housing cost

Add the items that arrive with the lease:

  • Utilities and internet
  • Renters' insurance (often required)
  • Parking or a transit pass
  • Furniture and the deposit's opportunity cost
  • Moving costs, amortised over how long you stay

A $1,800 apartment with $250 of utilities, $60 of internet and $25 of insurance is $2,135 a month — 85.4 hours at $25 an hour.

The trade-offs worth pricing

  • Commute. A cheaper apartment 45 minutes further away costs about 7.5 hours a month in travel time. At $25 an hour, that is $187.50 of time — often more than the rent saved.
  • A housemate. Splitting a $2,600 two-bedroom turns $2,600 into $1,300 each, saving 52 hours of work a month at $25 an hour.
  • A longer lease. Fixed rent for two years removes renewal risk; in a rising market that has real value even if the monthly figure is identical.

Work out your own number

Divide your monthly rent by your take-home hourly rate. To get your rate from a salary, see What Is an Hour of Your Time Worth? or use the cost-of-time calculator.

Assumptions: take-home pay, 12 equal monthly payments, constant costs. Not included: taxes, deposits, utilities, rent increases, or the tax treatment of homeownership.

HOW THESE NUMBERS ARE CALCULATED

Every figure on this page is arithmetic from stated assumptions: take-home pay, an 8-hour working day and 2,080 working hours a year (40 hours × 52 weeks), unless stated otherwise. Rates, taxes, fees and prices change, so treat the tables as a way to see the shape of a decision rather than a quote. Worth is a perspective tool, not financial advice.
