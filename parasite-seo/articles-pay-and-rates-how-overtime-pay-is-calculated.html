How Overtime Pay Is Calculated (Time-and-a-Half, Double Time)

How overtime is worked out, with tables at 1.5x and 2x rates, salaried vs hourly eligibility, and the effective hourly cost of unpaid overtime.

Overtime is usually paid at 1.5× your regular hourly rate ("time and a half") for hours beyond a threshold — commonly 40 hours in a workweek — and sometimes at 2× ("double time") beyond a higher threshold or on rest days.


Overtime pay = overtime hours × regular hourly rate × multiplier
Total pay = (regular hours × rate) + overtime pay

How much overtime adds to a salary

Weekly overtime at time-and-a-half adds 15% to a $41,600 salary. It is also 208 extra hours a year — about five working weeks.

Who is eligible

Eligibility depends on how you are classified, and the rules differ by country. In the United States, the Fair Labor Standards Act requires overtime at 1.5× for hours over 40 in a workweek for non-exempt employees; exempt status depends on duties and on a salary threshold that has been changed and litigated repeatedly, so check the current Department of Labor guidance rather than relying on any figure quoted in an article. Elsewhere, thresholds are set by local law, sector agreements or contract.

Two practical points that hold in most systems:

  • Being paid a salary does not automatically remove overtime rights. Classification usually depends on duties and pay thresholds.
  • "Comp time," unpaid overtime and flat-rate allowances are common and are not the same as overtime pay. Read what your contract actually says.

Special cases worth checking: shift differentials, on-call and standby time, travel time, and whether rest-day or public-holiday work carries a higher multiplier.

Questions to ask before accepting overtime

  1. Is the multiplier 1.5×, 2×, or flat rate?
  2. Is it voluntary or rostered, and can you decline?
  3. Does it affect next week's hours — will you be sent home early to balance the books?
  4. Are rest days or public holidays paid at a higher rate?
  5. Does overtime count toward pension contributions, bonuses or holiday accrual?

Assumptions: hourly rate, weekly threshold of 40 hours, multipliers of 1.5× and 2×, no shift differentials. Overtime law varies by country, state and contract — check the rules that apply to you. General information, not legal advice.
