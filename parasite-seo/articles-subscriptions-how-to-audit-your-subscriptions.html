How to Audit Your Subscriptions in 30 Minutes (Step-by-Step)

A 30-minute subscription audit that finds forgotten renewals and duplicates, with a scoring table, a keep-or-cancel rule and reusable templates.

A subscription audit is a 30-minute exercise with one output: a list of charges you have decided to keep and a list you have cancelled. Do it twice a year — once when you start, and once every six months afterwards.

What you need

  • 12 months of bank and card statements (not 2 — annual renewals hide in months 3 to 12)
  • Access to the email address you sign up with
  • A notes app or spreadsheet
  • 30 minutes

Step 1: build the list (12 minutes)

Search your statements for recurring merchants. Two passes catch more than one:

  1. Pass one: scan month by month for identical amounts.
  2. Pass two: sort by merchant name and look for repeats with irregular amounts (price rises, top-ups, annual renewals).

Record each one as: name, amount, frequency, last used, and category.

Step 2: find the hidden ones (6 minutes)

Three places hold subscriptions your bank statement does not label clearly:

  • App store subscriptions. On iOS, Settings → your name → Subscriptions. On Android, Google Play → Profile → Payments & subscriptions.
  • Payment processors. Search your email for "receipt," "renewal," "invoice" and "your subscription."
  • Bank standing orders and direct debits. Check the payments section of your banking app, not just the transactions list.

Step 3: score each one (6 minutes)

ScoreQuestionPoints
Used in the last 30 daysYes = 2, no = 0
Used weeklyYes = 3, sometimes = 1, no = 0
Would I re-buy today at this price?Yes = 3, unsure = 1, no = 0
Annual cost under $50?Yes = 2, no = 0

Total scoreDecision
8 to 10Keep at full price
4 to 7Keep, but check for a cheaper tier or annual plan
1 to 3Pause or cancel; re-subscribe if you miss it
0 or negativeCancel now

Step 4: check for cheaper tiers (3 minutes)

Before cancelling anything you use, check three things:

  • An ad-supported tier. Often 30–40% cheaper for the same catalogue.
  • An annual plan. Usually two months free, sometimes more. Compare annual price against monthly × 12 before switching — see Annual vs Monthly Subscription Plans.
  • A shared or family plan. See Family and Shared Plans: When Splitting Saves.

Step 5: cancel deliberately (3 minutes)

Cancel from the provider's own billing page where possible, then confirm by email. Set a calendar reminder three days before the next renewal date for anything you chose to keep, so a price rise does not renew silently — the subject of What to Do When a Subscription Raises Its Price.
