Ad revenue

Revenue = (Views ÷ 1,000,000) × RPM

RPM already accounts for the creator’s 55% share of the ad pool and ad fill rates.

Worked example

Assumptions: Views per month, with RPM set per niche.

NicheTypical RPM1M views earns

What the numbers do not tell you

  • Niche beats audience size. A finance channel with 200,000 subscribers can out-earn an entertainment channel with two million, because advertisers bid more per view.
  • The 55% creator share is fixed, but ad fill and CPM are not. Short videos and international audiences earn noticeably less per view.
  • Revenue splits by format: long-form ad revenue, Shorts fund revenue and sponsorships are separate, and Shorts pay a fraction of long-form.

Before you rely on this

RPM varies with season, audience location and advertiser demand — finance content can fall in January and rise in tax season.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good RPM for a new channel?

It depends entirely on the topic. Finance and software channels see $12–$30, entertainment $2–$5, and new channels should expect the lower end regardless of niche.

How many views do you need to make a living?

At a $5 RPM you need 4.8 million views a month for $20,000 before tax. In a $20 RPM niche it is 1.2 million.

Do sponsorships pay more than ads?

Usually. A channel with a modest but engaged audience can earn more from two sponsorships than from months of ad revenue, and sponsorship income is also more predictable.
