How to Cancel a Subscription (and Ask for a Refund) in 2026

Step-by-step cancellation for app store, website, carrier and bundled subscriptions, plus a refund request template and what to do when a provider refuses.

Cancel through the channel you subscribed in, keep the confirmation, and ask for a refund before the next billing date. Most subscription disputes are won or lost on whether you cancelled through the right channel — cancelling a card is not a cancellation.

Step 1: find where you subscribed

The billing channel matters more than the service. A subscription started inside an iPhone app is billed by Apple, not by the service.

Sign-up routeWho to cancel with
iPhone or iPad appApple: Settings → your name → Subscriptions
Android appGoogle Play: Profile → Payments & subscriptions → Subscriptions
Provider's websiteThat provider's account or billing page
Mobile phone billYour carrier, or the provider if it manages the billing
Bundle (bank, telecoms, hardware)The company that sold the bundle
Gift card or prepaidUsually expires by itself; check the terms

Step 2: cancel, then verify (not just "cancel")

  1. Open the subscription list and select the service.
  2. Choose the cancel or "turn off renewal" option. Confirm any retention offer you decline — declining is not rude, and it is not a cancellation.
  3. Screenshot the confirmation screen and save the confirmation email.
  4. Write down the access end date. Many plans keep access until the paid period ends, which is not the same as being cancelled.
  5. Check your statement after the next billing date. A charge after cancellation is the signal to contact support with your screenshot.

Step 3: ask for a refund

Refunds are discretionary in most consumer contracts, but three arguments work consistently:

  • Recency. A charge from today or yesterday, on an account with no use since renewal.
  • Unused service. Providers can see usage; an unused month is an easy refund.
  • A billing error. Charged after cancelling, charged twice, or charged at a price that was not the one displayed.

Refund request template

Subject: Refund request — [service name], charge dated [date], [amount]

Hello,

I was charged [amount] on [date] for [service/plan]. I cancelled my subscription on [date] (confirmation attached) / I have not used the service since the renewal / the price charged does not match the price shown at signup.

Please cancel the renewal and refund the [amount] charge. If a partial refund is the only option available, please let me know what period that covers.

Account email: [email]

Thank you.

Step 4: if the provider refuses

OptionWhen to use itWhat to expect
Reply once with the confirmation attachedProvider claims no cancellationOften resolved; support rarely sees the first message's attachment
Ask for the complaints or escalation teamFirst-line refusalSlower, higher authority
Card chargeback through your bankCharge after cancellation, or service not deliveredBank decides; provide the confirmation and dates
Consumer protection bodyRegulated markets, unresolved disputeFree, slow, sometimes binding on the provider
Small claims courtLarge, clearly documented amountsLast resort; check local limits

Chargeback rules and consumer rights differ by country and by card network. In some regions, cancelling a direct debit without telling the provider can create a debt rather than end one — cancel with the provider first.

Special cases

  • Annual plans. Often non-refundable by contract; asking anyway is free, and a recently renewed unused year is the most sympathetic case.
  • Free trials that converted. Usually the easiest refund to win if you ask within days — see Free Trials That Turn Into Subscriptions.
  • Gyms and local services. Frequently require written notice with 30 to 60 days' notice; check the contract, not the app.
  • Cancelled but still charged. This is a billing error, not a dispute. Send the confirmation and the charge date together.

After cancelling: stop the cycle repeating

The cheapest subscription is the one that never starts. Keep a single list of renewal dates, audit twice a year with How to Audit Your Subscriptions in 30 Minutes, and put the annual cost of anything new through the subscription calculator before you subscribe.

Assumptions: general consumer guidance, not legal advice. Consumer cancellation and refund rights vary significantly by country and by contract; check the terms you agreed to and the rules where you live.
