## What is The small purchases that aren’t actually small?

 Small is a frequency, too  A $5 coffee is a $5 coffee. Every day for a year, it is $1,825. Neither framing is more correct, but seeing both helps you make a choice instead of following a habit.  Look at value, not just price  Does that coffee give you a quiet moment before a difficult day? Is it a chance to meet a friend? The benefit matters. A daily purchase you love can be a better use of money than an unused subscription that costs less.  Try one realistic change  Skipping a $5 coffee twice a week would leave $520 over 52 weeks. That is different from cutting it out entirely. Look for a change you would actually enjoy keeping, rather than an ambitious plan you would resent.  Keep the assumptions visible  Our small habits calculator assumes a daily amount across 365 days. It does not assume investment returns or interest. Your actual savings depend on how often you make the change and whether you spend the difference elsewhere.
