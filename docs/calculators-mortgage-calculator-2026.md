## How many hours of work a mortgage payment costs

Hours per month = monthly payment ÷ take-home hourly pay. A $2,200 payment at $30 per hour after tax is 73 hours, or about 42% of a 173-hour working month. Lenders talk about debt-to-income; this is the same idea expressed in time.

| Loan amount (6.5%, 30 yr) | P&I per month | Hours at $30/hr |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $250,000 | $1,580 | 53 h |
| $350,000 | $2,212 | 74 h |
| $450,000 | $2,844 | 95 h |
| $600,000 | $3,792 | 126 h |

## Estimating the payment first

For a fixed-rate loan the monthly principal-and-interest payment is P × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)−n), where r is the monthly rate and n the number of payments. At 6.5% over 30 years, every $100,000 borrowed costs about $632 a month. Add property tax and insurance before dividing by your hourly pay.

## What the hours figure leaves out

It ignores equity you build, tax treatment, and rent you would otherwise pay. Use it to compare two homes or a house against renting at the same hourly rate, not as an affordability rule on its own.

## FAQ

**How do I convert a mortgage payment to hours of work?**

Divide the full monthly payment (principal, interest, tax, insurance) by your after-tax hourly pay. $2,200 ÷ $30 = 73 hours a month.

**What share of my working month should go to housing?**

Common guidance keeps housing under 28–30% of gross income, which is roughly 50–60 hours of a 173-hour month at take-home rates. Your other debts and savings goals matter more than the rule.

**Does this include property tax and insurance?**

Only if you add them to the payment you enter. Escrowed tax and insurance often add 20–30% to the principal-and-interest figure.
