How to Do a Subscription Audit in One Hour

A step-by-step subscription audit: find every recurring charge, decide keep or cancel, and set up guardrails so they never creep back.

September 27, 2026

A subscription audit is a one-hour sweep of every recurring charge you pay — streaming, apps, boxes, memberships, "free trials" that stopped being free — with a keep/cancel decision on each. Most households find several hundred dollars a year of charges they've stopped using, plus a few price increases nobody noticed.

Why subscriptions need an audit, not a glance

Three properties make subscriptions uniquely leaky: they're small per unit (below the decision threshold), automatic (no purchase moment = no review moment), and creeping (prices step up quietly, tiers rebrand, trials convert). Your memory of "my subscriptions" is almost always lower than the bank statement's version.

The one-hour procedure

Minutes 0–15: find every charge

  • Open the last three months of bank and credit card statements. Search recurring patterns: same merchant, similar amount, monthly or yearly.
  • Check app stores (Apple/Google subscriptions live in one settings screen) — mobile-billing subscriptions are the ones people forget entirely.
  • Check PayPal's automatic payments list and any direct debits.
  • Write everything in one list: name, amount, cycle, last used.

Minutes 15–45: judge each one honestly

For each subscription ask three questions:

  1. Used in the last 30 days? (Not "could be useful" — used.)
  2. Would I re-subscribe today at this price?
  3. Is there overlap? Two music services, three video platforms, overlapping cloud storage — one must go.

Verdicts: Keep (genuinely used), Downgrade (tier too rich — music at the paid tier you don't need, storage 2TB you use 100GB of), Pause (most streamers allow it; the show returns in six months and so can you), Cancel.

Minutes 45–60: execute and armor

  • Cancel via the service's own settings (email-only cancellation is illegal in many jurisdictions now; if a company buries it, a written cancellation email with date is your record).
  • Screenshot confirmations. Date-stamp them.
  • For keepers: turn OFF one-click where renewals might tempt future-you, note renewal dates, and check if annual billing is cheaper than monthly.

Guardrails so it doesn't leak back

  1. Calendar the audit. Every 6 months — birthday month works as a memory hook.
  2. Trial policy: cancel immediately after starting, or set a cancel-date reminder for 2 days before the trial ends. Services that auto-convert count on you forgetting.
  3. The replacement rule: a new subscription requires removing one, or a written justification in your notes.
  4. Bank-level tooling: some banks and card issuers surface recurring merchants in-app — use the view if yours has it. Budget apps with subscription detection do the same.
  5. Share tier economics: family plans split across a household often halve per-person cost — but audit shared ones too; shared subscriptions outlive individual use.

Frequently asked questions

Should I cancel and re-subscribe later?

Usually yes — content libraries cycle, and most services keep your profile for months. The "I might watch it someday" insurance policy is exactly the reasoning that keeps $600/year alive; the re-subscribe button costs nothing.

What about annual subscriptions I've paid already?

The money is sunk — decide forward. If you wouldn't buy the year today, cancel before it auto-renews (that's the real deadline) and use what remains of the paid period, or request a prorated refund where policy allows.

Are free trials worth it?

For genuinely-evaluated products, yes — with a calendar cancel-date set the moment you sign up. A free trial without a cancel reminder is a paid subscription with a delayed invoice.

Does cancelling mid-cycle waste money?

Check whether the service offers prorated refunds; many digital ones don't. In the common no-refund case, the rational choice is to cancel now for the next renewal and use the remainder of the current period — waiting until the day before renewal only risks forgetting.
