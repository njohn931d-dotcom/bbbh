# Concert Ticket Cost in Hours of Work

## All-in cost, not face value

Trip cost = tickets + fees + travel + hotel + food + merch. Eras Tour face values ran $49–$449, but resale averaged $1,000–$2,500 and the average attendee spent about $1,300 on the full trip. At $30 per hour that is 43 hours of work for a three-hour show.

| Line item | Typical 2026 cost | Hours at $30/hr |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ticket (resale, lower bowl) | $900 | 30 h |
| Fees | $150 | 5 h |
| Travel + hotel (1 night) | $400 | 13 h |
| Merch + food | $150 | 5 h |
| Total | $1,600 | 53 h |

## Cost per hour of show

Divide total cost by show length. $1,300 ÷ 3.5 hours = $371 per hour of music, or 12 working hours for every hour in the stadium. Comparing that to a $20 movie ($10 per hour) clarifies what you are buying: the experience, not the time.

## Making it cheaper

Face-value resale queues, weekday or secondary-city dates, driving instead of flying, and skipping the $80 hoodie each cut the total by 10–40%. Decide the hours budget first, then fit the trip into it.

## FAQ

**How much do Taylor Swift tickets cost?**

Face value $49–$449 on the Eras Tour; resale prices commonly ran $1,000–$2,500 and higher for floor seats.

**What did fans spend in total?**

Surveys put the average all-in trip cost near $1,300 including travel, lodging, and merch.

**How do I decide if a concert is worth it?**

Convert the all-in cost to hours of work and compare with other things you would do with those hours.
