How to calculate the work hours behind a purchase

Work hours = purchase price ÷ take-home hourly pay. A $150 purchase at $25 per hour takes 6 hours of work. This number is a different way to look at spending, not a judgment about what you should buy. Worth's open-source calculator runs 100% in your browser - source on GitHub.

Converting a monthly or annual salary

For a 40-hour week over 52 weeks, annual work time is 2,080 hours. Divide annual take-home pay by 2,080, or monthly take-home pay by 173.33. Someone taking home $52,000 per year has an estimated $25 hourly rate. If you work different hours, enter your actual hourly pay instead.

What this calculation leaves out

It does not account for rent, bills, savings obligations, or the emotional value of a purchase. Your full take-home wage is not all disposable income. Use the result as perspective, not as an affordability assessment.

How does the cost of time calculator work?+

Divide the price of a purchase by your take-home hourly pay. A $150 purchase at $25 per hour costs 6 hours of work. For monthly income, we use 173.33 working hours per month; for annual income, 2,080 hours per year. These assume a 40-hour workweek over 52 weeks. If your schedule differs, enter your actual hourly take-home pay instead.

Should I use my salary before or after tax?+

Use after-tax pay—the money that actually reaches your bank account. For a more personal estimate, divide your take-home pay by the actual hours you work, including unpaid work time if you choose.

How are subscriptions and small savings calculated?+

Subscription check multiplies a monthly cost by 12, then divides the annual total by your hourly pay. Small habits multiplies a daily expense by 365 to show a possible yearly saving. Neither calculation includes investment returns, inflation, or price changes.

Do you store my financial information?+

Your calculations run in your browser. We don’t send your input to a server. Saved thoughts live in this browser’s local storage and can be deleted at any time. Share links include the calculator values in the URL fragment, so only share numbers you’re comfortable disclosing.
