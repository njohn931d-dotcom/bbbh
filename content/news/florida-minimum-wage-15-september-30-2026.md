Florida's minimum wage rises from $14.00 to **$15.00** an hour on September 30, 2026. It is the last of the yearly $1 steps written into the state constitution by Amendment 2, which voters approved in 2020. The U.S. Department of Labor's [state table](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/minimum-wage/state), updated on July 1, still showed $14.00 because the final step had not yet taken effect.

## What the last step adds

::wagestep 14 15

For a full-time worker that is $40 more a week and $2,080 more a year before tax.

## Where Florida ranks now

With $15.00, Florida is one of {{wages.at15States}} states, plus the District of Columbia, with a minimum wage of $15 or more. {{wages.atFloor}} states remain at the federal {{wages.federal}}, and {{wages.noLawCount}} have no state minimum wage law at all. The highest rate anywhere in the Department of Labor's table is the District of Columbia at $18.40; Washington is the highest of the 50 states at $17.13.

See every state ranked on the [minimum wage by state](/minimum-wage/) page, or read the [Florida page](/minimum-wage/florida/) for overtime rules and a take-home estimate.

## What it means for the price of things

Time is the real price. At $14.00 an hour, a full-time worker's estimated take-home is about $12.30 an hour, so a $100 purchase takes about 8.1 hours of work. At $15.00 the take-home is about $13.10 an hour and the same purchase takes about 7.6 hours. The raise gives back roughly half an hour on every $100 spent.

## After this step

Florida is listed among the states that [index the minimum wage to inflation](https://www.hivedesk.com/blog/outsourcing/minimum-wage-by-state), so later changes will follow a formula instead of a fixed schedule. Check the Florida Department of Commerce for the rate announced for the next increase.

## If you are paid hourly in Florida

- Check that the pay stub covering September 30 and after reflects at least $15.00 an hour.
- Local rules, union contracts and your employer may pay more than the state minimum.
