A federal deduction for overtime pay, part of the tax law signed on July 4, 2025, applies to tax years 2025 through 2028. It lets eligible workers deduct up to **$12,500** of qualified overtime compensation from federal taxable income, or **$25,000** on a joint return, [Fidelity explains](https://www.fidelity.com/learning-center/personal-finance/no-tax-on-overtime). The deduction starts to phase out at a modified adjusted gross income of $150,000 ($300,000 joint) and shrinks by $100 for every $1,000 above that.

## What counts as qualified overtime

Only overtime that the Fair Labor Standards Act requires qualifies, meaning time-and-a-half for hours over 40 in a workweek. The deduction covers the premium portion, the extra half on top of your regular rate, not the whole overtime paycheck. According to [Grimbleby Coleman](https://grimbleby-coleman.com/the-no-tax-on-overtime-deduction-explained/), overtime paid only under state law, a union contract or an employer policy does not increase the deductible amount if the FLSA does not require it. Eligibility is limited to non-exempt employees covered by the FLSA, and married people filing separately cannot claim it, according to [PYA](https://www.pyapc.com/insights/no-tax-on-overtime-what-the-2025-2028-federal-deduction-really-means/) and [RemoteLaws](https://remotelaws.com/federal/no-tax-on-overtime/). [Paycor](https://www.paycor.com/resource-center/articles/no-tax-on-overtime/) confirms the 2025 to 2028 window.

A worker earning $25 an hour with 10 overtime hours a week deducts $6,250 and saves about $1,175. The same overtime at $18 an hour saves less, because more of it is taxed at the lower 12% rate.

## What it does not do

::note The deduction lowers **federal income tax** only. Social Security and Medicare tax still apply to every overtime dollar, and state and local income taxes may too.

- It is **not** an exemption for the whole overtime paycheck, only the premium half.
- Above the phase-out, the deduction shrinks by $100 for every $1,000 of income over $150,000 ($300,000 joint), and it is gone entirely at $275,000 ($550,000 joint).
- It reduces taxable income, not your reported wages, so your pay stub will not change.
