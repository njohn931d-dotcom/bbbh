Apple announced the iPhone 18 Pro and iPhone 18 Pro Max on September 9, 2026, at **$1,199** and **$1,299** for the 256 GB models, according to [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/09/09/iphone-18-pro-pricing/). That is $100 more than the equivalent iPhone 17 Pro models. Pre-orders opened on September 12 and the phones went on sale on September 18, [The Apple Post](https://www.theapplepost.com/2026/09/09/71932/iphone-18-pro-starts-at-1199-as-apple-raises-pro-pricing-by-100/) reports.

MacRumors ties the increase to a broader run of 2026 price rises across Apple's Mac, iPad and home lines, which it links to the global memory shortage. It also notes that a trade-in of an iPhone 14 or newer can take up to $1,200 off, so the price you actually pay may be lower.

## Cost per day of use

The price matters less than how long you keep the phone. Spread over the years you use it, the $1,199 works out like this, before any trade-in value:

| Years kept | Cost per month | Cost per day |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2 | $49.96 | $1.64 |
| 3 | $33.31 | $1.09 |
| 4 | $24.98 | $0.82 |
| 5 | $19.98 | $0.66 |

Keeping the phone for four years instead of two halves the daily cost, from $1.64 to $0.82.

## Questions worth asking before you upgrade

- **What does my current phone not do that I need?** If the answer is nothing, the upgrade is a want, and that is fine, but call it that.
- **What is my current phone worth?** Apple advertises up to $1,200 for an iPhone 14 or newer. Real values depend on model and condition.
- **Would a cheaper or used model do the job?** The [cost per use guides](/articles/spending-decisions/) show how to compare.
- **Am I paying over time?** Financing spreads the same hours of work across more months. It does not reduce them.
