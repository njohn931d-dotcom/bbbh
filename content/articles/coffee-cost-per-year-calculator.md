A $5 coffee every day for a year costs **$1,825**. On workdays only (about 260 days), the same habit costs $1,300. At a $25 take-home hourly rate, the daily version is 73 hours of work a year.

Coffee is the most-quoted example of small spending for a reason: it is frequent, it is optional, and it is easy to count. Here is the arithmetic, done honestly.

## Yearly cost by cup price

| Cup price | Workdays only (260) | Every day (365) | Work hours at $25/h (365-day) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $3.00 | $780 | $1,095 | 43.8 |
| $4.00 | $1,040 | $1,460 | 58.4 |
| $5.00 | $1,300 | $1,825 | 73.0 |
| $5.50 | $1,430 | $2,007.50 | 80.3 |

## What brewing at home saves

Home-brewed coffee costs roughly $0.50–$0.80 a cup depending on beans and equipment. At $0.60 against a $5.00 cafe cup, every cup saves $4.40:

| Schedule | Cafe cost/year | Home cost/year | Saved/year | Work hours saved at $25/h |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Workdays (260) | $1,300 | $156 | $1,144 | 45.8 |
| Every day (365) | $1,825 | $219 | $1,606 | 64.2 |
| Twice a week (104) | $520 | $62.40 | $457.60 | 18.3 |

## Why the frequency matters more than the price

Dropping from a $7 drink to a $5 drink saves $730 a year if you buy daily. Dropping from daily to twice a week saves $1,305 a year at the same $5 price. Cutting frequency beats trading down.

## The honest caveats

- **Saving is not automatic.** The money only becomes savings if it actually moves somewhere. Otherwise it disappears into other spending.
- **The habit may be worth it.** If the coffee is how you start your workday, see a colleague, or get out of the house, it is buying something beyond caffeine.
- **Interest is not included.** These are plain totals, not investment projections.

## How to work out your own number

1. Count how many cafe drinks you buy in a normal week (be honest — check a bank statement).
2. Multiply by your average cup price, then by 52.
3. Subtract the cost of whatever you would drink instead.
4. Divide by your take-home hourly rate to see the total in work hours.

The [daily savings calculator](/calculators/daily-savings/) does steps 2 to 4 for any daily amount, and the [cost-of-time calculator](/calculators/cost-of-time/) converts a single purchase into hours.

Assumptions: 260 weekdays or 365 days, constant prices, no interest or inflation, home-brew at $0.60 a cup.
