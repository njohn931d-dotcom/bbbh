Renting and buying are not comparable on monthly payment alone. Renting buys flexibility and predictability; buying buys stability and an asset — and carries maintenance, transaction costs and risk. The deciding variable is usually **how long you will stay**.

## The two cost stacks

| Renting costs | Buying costs |
| --- | --- |
| Rent | Mortgage interest (not principal repayment) |
| Renters' insurance | Property taxes and insurance |
| Utilities | Maintenance and repairs (typically 1–2% of value a year) |
| Moving costs | HOA or service charges |
| Rent increases | Closing costs on purchase and sale |
| Nothing else | Opportunity cost of the deposit |

The principal portion of a mortgage payment is not a cost — it is saving. The interest, tax, insurance, maintenance and transaction costs are the real cost of owning.

## Worked example

$350,000 home, 20% deposit ($70,000), 30-year mortgage at 6.5%. Comparable rent: $2,000 a month.

| Renting (monthly) | | Buying (monthly) | |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Rent | $2,000 | Principal + interest | $1,771 |
| Renters' insurance | $25 | Property tax | $292 |
| Utilities | $180 | Insurance | $125 |
| | | Maintenance (1.5%/yr) | $438 |
| | | HOA | $120 |
| **Monthly cost** | **$2,205** | **Monthly cost** | **$2,746** |
| **Monthly *true* cost** | **$2,205** | (excluding the ~$343 principal saving, which is forced saving rather than cost) | **$2,403** |

Buying costs roughly $200 a month more before any appreciation — plus a $70,000 deposit that could be earning interest elsewhere, and closing costs of perhaps $7,000–$14,000.

## Break-even horizon

| Factor | Renting advantage | Buying advantage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Upfront cash | Deposit stays invested | — |
| Monthly cost | Often lower in years 1–5 | Falls in real terms as rent rises |
| Maintenance | Landlord's problem | 1–2% of value a year |
| Transaction costs | One month's deposit | 2–5% in, 6–10% out in many markets |
| Flexibility | Leave at lease end | Sale takes months and costs thousands |
| Predictability | Rent can rise | Fixed-rate payments are stable |
| Appreciation | None | Accumulates, and is not guaranteed |

Rough rule of thumb: **transaction costs are recovered over several years, not one.** With 8% total transaction costs on a $350,000 home ($28,000), you need roughly $28,000 of advantage — from appreciation, rent inflation avoided, or a lower monthly cost — before buying wins. At a $200 monthly advantage to renting, that is about 12 years before other factors close the gap; at 3% annual rent inflation, the crossover comes much sooner.

## Five-year total comparison

| | Renting | Buying |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly outlay | $2,205 | $2,746 |
| Five-year outlay | $132,300 | $164,760 |
| Less principal repaid | $0 | −$22,900 |
| Less appreciation (0%) | $0 | $0 |
| Plus closing costs | $0 | +$10,500 |
| Plus deposit opportunity cost (4% on $70,000) | $0 | +$14,000 |
| **Five-year net cost** | **$132,300** | **$166,360** |

At 0% appreciation, renting wins by about $34,000 over five years. At 4% annual appreciation, the home adds roughly $75,800 of value over the same period and buying wins decisively. **The entire answer hinges on appreciation and how long you stay — neither of which is knowable in advance.**

## The five questions that actually decide it

1. **How long will you stay?** Under three to five years, renting usually wins on transaction costs alone.
2. **Is your income stable?** A mortgage is a 30-year commitment; renting can end at the lease break.
3. **Do you have a deposit *and* a reserve?** Buying with no emergency fund converts any repair into debt — see [How Big Should My Emergency Fund Be?](/articles/saving-habits/how-big-should-my-emergency-fund-be/).
4. **How does your local rent-to-price ratio look?** Compare annual rent to property price; a 5% ratio favours renting, a 10% ratio favours buying.
5. **Are you buying flexibility or an asset?** Both are legitimate — but only one of them is a financial argument.

## What buyers forget to budget

| Cost | Typical amount | Frequency |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Closing and legal fees | 2 – 5% of price | At purchase |
| Moving | $1,000 – $5,000 | At purchase |
| Immediate repairs and furniture | $3,000 – $15,000 | Year one |
| Maintenance and replacements | 1 – 2% of value a year | Ongoing |
| Selling costs | 6 – 10% of sale price in some markets (agent fees, taxes, transfer) | At sale |

## A middle path

- **Rent while you save**, then buy with a larger deposit and a better rate.
- **Rent in an expensive area, buy where it is cheaper**, if your work allows it.
- **Buy something smaller than you can afford**, so the payment is not the largest line in your budget.
