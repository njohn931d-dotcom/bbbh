A subscription audit is a 30-minute exercise with one output: a list of charges you have decided to keep and a list you have cancelled. Do it twice a year — once when you start, and once every six months afterwards.

## What you need

- 12 months of bank and card statements (not 2 — annual renewals hide in months 3 to 12)
- Access to the email address you sign up with
- A notes app or spreadsheet
- 30 minutes

## Step 1: build the list (12 minutes)

Search your statements for recurring merchants. Two passes catch more than one:

1. **Pass one:** scan month by month for identical amounts.
2. **Pass two:** sort by merchant name and look for repeats with irregular amounts (price rises, top-ups, annual renewals).

Record each one as: name, amount, frequency, last used, and category.

## Step 2: find the hidden ones (6 minutes)

Three places hold subscriptions your bank statement does not label clearly:

- **App store subscriptions.** On iOS, Settings → your name → Subscriptions. On Android, Google Play → Profile → Payments & subscriptions.
- **Payment processors.** Search your email for "receipt," "renewal," "invoice" and "your subscription."
- **Bank standing orders and direct debits.** Check the payments section of your banking app, not just the transactions list.

## Step 3: score each one (6 minutes)

| Score | Question | Points |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Used in the last 30 days | Yes = 2, no = 0 | |
| Used weekly | Yes = 3, sometimes = 1, no = 0 | |
| Would I re-buy today at this price? | Yes = 3, unsure = 1, no = 0 | |
| Annual cost under $50? | Yes = 2, no = 0 | |
| Duplicates something else I pay for | Yes = −3, no = 0 | |

| Total score | Decision |
| --- | --- |
| 8 to 10 | Keep at full price |
| 4 to 7 | Keep, but check for a cheaper tier or annual plan |
| 1 to 3 | Pause or cancel; re-subscribe if you miss it |
| 0 or negative | Cancel now |

## Step 4: check for cheaper tiers (3 minutes)

Before cancelling anything you use, check three things:

- **An ad-supported tier.** Often 30–40% cheaper for the same catalogue.
- **An annual plan.** Usually two months free, sometimes more. Compare annual price against monthly × 12 before switching — see [Annual vs Monthly Subscription Plans](/articles/subscriptions/annual-vs-monthly-subscription-plan/).
- **A shared or family plan.** See [Family and Shared Plans: When Splitting Saves](/articles/subscriptions/family-and-shared-plans-when-splitting-saves/).

## Step 5: cancel deliberately (3 minutes)

Cancel from the provider's own billing page where possible, then confirm by email. Set a calendar reminder three days before the next renewal date for anything you chose to keep, so a price rise does not renew silently — the subject of [What to Do When a Subscription Raises Its Price](/articles/subscriptions/subscription-price-increase-what-to-do/).

## After the audit: keep it from drifting back

| Habit | Effort | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One dedicated card for subscriptions | Low | Every recurring charge visible in one list |
| A recurring 30-minute audit every 6 months | Low | Catches trials that converted |
| Email folder for receipts | Low | Makes renewal notices findable |
| Check the renewal date before any free trial | Low | Prevents the charge you did not plan for |

## Work out what you are actually paying

Enter your total monthly subscription cost into the [subscription calculator](/calculators/subscription-cost/) to see the annual figure and the hours of work it represents. For most people, the annual number is the one that changes the decision.

Assumptions: consumer subscriptions only; business expenses, phone contracts and insurance are usually tracked separately.
