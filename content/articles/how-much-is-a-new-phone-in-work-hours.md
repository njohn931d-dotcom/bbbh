A $1,000 phone costs **40 hours of work** at a $25 take-home hourly rate, or 50 hours at $20. Spread over three years of use, the same phone costs about 1.1 hours of work per month.

Sticker price is the least interesting number on a phone. What matters is the price per month of use and how many hours of work it takes to replace it.

## Flagship vs mid-range in work hours

| Phone price | Hours at $15/h | $20/h | $25/h | $30/h | $40/h |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $499 (mid-range) | 33.3 | 25.0 | 20.0 | 16.6 | 12.5 |
| $799 | 53.3 | 40.0 | 32.0 | 26.6 | 20.0 |
| $999 (flagship) | 66.6 | 50.0 | 40.0 | 33.3 | 25.0 |

| $1,599 (foldable) | 106.6 | 80.0 | 64.0 | 53.3 | 40.0 |

## The number that actually decides it: cost per month

Divide the price by how many months you will keep the phone.

| Phone price | 24 months | 36 months | 48 months |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $499 | $20.79/mo | $13.86/mo | $10.40/mo |
| $999 | $41.63/mo | $27.75/mo | $20.81/mo |
| $1,199 | $49.96/mo | $33.31/mo | $24.98/mo |

A $1,000 phone kept for four years ($20.81 a month) is cheaper per month than a $500 phone replaced every two years ($20.79 a month is level with it — and the older flagship will feel slower by year four). **Replacement cycle is the variable you control.**

## Financing: what a 24-month plan really costs

Carrier instalments quote a monthly figure that hides the total. Multiply the monthly amount by the number of months, then add the plan cost:

```
Total cost = (monthly device charge × months) + (monthly plan × months)
```

| Device charge | Months | Device total | Plus $45/mo plan | All-in total | Work hours at $25/h |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $20.83 | 24 | $500 | $1,080 | $1,580 | 63.2 |
| $41.63 | 24 | $999 | $1,080 | $2,079 | 83.2 |
| $49.96 | 24 | $1,199 | $1,080 | $2,279 | 91.2 |

Phone plan prices vary widely by country, data allowance and promotions; substitute your own figure before deciding.

## Upgrading annually vs every three years

| Upgrade cycle | Phones bought in 6 years | Cost at $999 | Work hours at $25/h |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Every year | 6 | $5,994 | 239.8 |
| Every 2 years | 3 | $2,997 | 119.9 |
| Every 3 years | 2 | $1,998 | 79.9 |

The gap between annual and three-year upgrading is about 160 hours of work over six years — four working weeks. Trade-in values and repair costs can narrow that gap, so add your own resale estimate.

## A quick rule for your next purchase

Work out the price per month of ownership (price ÷ months kept), then ask whether that monthly figure buys something you would happily subscribe to. If the answer is yes at $27.75 a month but no at $41.63, you have found your upgrade cycle.

The same price-per-month logic applies to everything else you buy — see [Cost Per Use: The Only Fair Way to Compare Two Purchases](/articles/spending-decisions/cost-per-use-how-to-compare-purchases/), and [How Many Hours of Work Is $1,000?](/articles/work-hours/how-many-hours-of-work-is-1000-dollars/) for the flagship price in work time at more pay levels.

Assumptions: device cost only, no insurance, repairs, accessories, financing interest, trade-in value, or inflation. Hourly figures use take-home pay.
