Round-up apps work by moving the spare change from each card purchase into savings. The average round-up is about **$0.50 per transaction** (rounding is uniformly distributed between $0.01 and $0.99), so the annual total depends almost entirely on how often you spend on card.

At 40 card transactions a month, that is roughly $240 a year. Honest, but modest.

## What round-ups actually save

Average round-up of $0.50 per transaction:

| Transactions/month | Monthly saving | Annual saving | Work hours/year at $25/h |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 10 | $5 | $60 | 2.4 |
| 20 | $10 | $120 | 4.8 |
| 30 | $15 | $180 | 7.2 |
| 40 | $20 | $240 | 9.6 |
| 60 | $30 | $360 | 14.4 |
| 100 | $50 | $600 | 24.0 |

With a wider spread of round-ups (average $0.35 low, $0.65 high):

| Average round-up | 30 tx/month | 40 tx/month | 60 tx/month | Annual at 40 tx |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $0.35 | $10.50 | $14 | $21 | $168 |
| $0.50 | $15 | $20 | $30 | $240 |
| $0.65 | $19.50 | $26 | $39 | $312 |

## What changes the result: multipliers

Many apps let you multiply each round-up. This is where the numbers become meaningful:

| Multiplier | Monthly at 40 tx | Annual | Work hours/year at $25/h |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1× | $20 | $240 | 9.6 |
| 2× | $40 | $480 | 19.2 |
| 3× | $60 | $720 | 28.8 |
| 5× | $100 | $1,200 | 48.0 |
| 10× | $200 | $2,400 | 96.0 |

A 3× multiplier on 40 transactions a month saves $720 a year — comparable to a deliberate $60-a-month savings plan. Note what has happened: at that point the round-up is no longer spare change, it is a savings rate wearing a friendly interface. Which is fine, as long as you know it.

## Compare it to a plain transfer

| Method | Annual saving on 40 tx/month | Effort | Predictable |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Round-up 1× | $240 | None | No |
| Round-up 3× | $720 | None | No |
| $20/month standing order | $240 | One setup | Yes |
| $60/month standing order | $720 | One setup | Yes |
| 1% of take-home on payday | Varies | One setup | Yes |

The round-up advantage is not the amount — it is that it happens without a decision. For people who have never managed a monthly transfer, that is worth more than the difference between $240 and $720.

## What to check before using one

| Question | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Is there a monthly fee? | A $3 fee on a $20 monthly saving is a 15% deduction |
| Is the savings account insured where you live? | Protection limits and schemes vary by country |
| Does it earn interest? | Some savings pots pay nothing |
| Can you withdraw instantly? | Friction on the way out is a feature, until you need the money |
| Does it round up on every card or one linked card? | Determines your transaction count |
| What happens to failed round-ups? | Overdraft risk if a round-up pushes a balance negative |

A fee that looks small against a large balance can be large against a small one. At a $3 monthly fee, saving $20 a month costs 15% of the amount saved.

## Who round-ups suit

**Good fit:** people who spend on card often, have never automated savings, and want the barrier to starting to be zero.

**Poor fit:** people who mostly use cash, who already save a fixed monthly amount, or who pay a fee that eats a meaningful share of a small saving.

## Making the method stronger

1. **Add a weekly fixed amount** on top of round-ups — the transfer does the heavy lifting.
2. **Add a multiplier** once the round-up alone feels painless.
3. **Sweep the pot monthly** into a named goal so it does not get spent on nothing.
4. **Review after three months.** If the annual figure is under $150 and you want more, switch to a standing order — see [How Much Should I Save Each Month?](/articles/saving-habits/how-much-should-i-save-each-month/).

Assumptions: uniform distribution of cents, average round-up $0.50, no fees unless stated, no interest, investment returns or inflation. App features, fees and deposit protection vary by provider and country.
