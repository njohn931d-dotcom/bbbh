A money date is a scheduled, calm conversation about finances — for couples, a shared review; for solo, a formal solo check-in — typically 30 minutes, once or twice a month, with snacks allowed and blame forbidden. It's the forum where money *decisions* get made so that money *arguments* stop happening in the kitchen.

## Why couples need a calendar slot for this

Money conflicts in relationships are rarely about arithmetic — they're about information gaps and unspoken expectations. One partner tracks everything; the other hasn't seen a balance in months. One plans for security; one plans for the weekend. Without a shared ritual, these differences surface at the worst moment: mid-purchase, post-statement, during stress. A recurring money date moves the conversation to a neutral time with agreed rules — and couples who review finances together consistently report fewer money fights and higher shared confidence, even when incomes differ.

## The 30-minute agenda

**Before (5 min each):** both partners pull current numbers — balances, recent spending, upcoming big items. No surprises on screen.

**The running section:**
1. **Wins first (5 min).** Something that went right: debt dropped, savings hit a number, a planned purchase executed cleanly. Start positive; money talks that open with criticism end early.
2. **The scoreboard (5 min).** Savings rate this month, goal progress (visual: the chart or the thermometer). Just facts.
3. **What's coming (5 min).** Calendar scan: trips, birthdays, insurance renewals, school costs. Assign each to a sinking fund or decide to create one.
4. **Decisions (10 min).** One or two live questions max: *furnace repair or vacation fund priority? Raise the savings rate? Subscription verdict?* Write down what you decided and who does what.
5. **Close (5 min).** Agree the next date; one appreciation about money teamwork.

**Rule of the house:** either person can call a pause if the talk heats up — resume within 48 hours, same agenda.

## Solo money dates

Same structure, smaller: 15 minutes on payday — balances, upcoming, one decision, one win. The formalization matters: people who "think about money all the time" ruminate; people with a scheduled 15 minutes act.

## Conversation starters that go somewhere

- What's one purchase from this month we both still feel good about?
- If we got an unexpected $2,000, what would we do — and do we agree?
- Which recurring charge would we cancel if we saw it fresh today?
- Are we on track for the next 90 days of known costs?
- What does "enough" look like for us this year — where do we want to be by December?

Avoid reopening resolved arguments; the date is for decisions, not trials.

## Keeping it from dying

- **Anchor it:** same week each month, tied to something fixed (after rent clears, first Sunday).
- **Make it pleasant:** actual coffee, pastries, a walk — association matters; dread kills rituals.
- **Rotate the facilitator** in couples so it never feels like one person's lecture.
- **Keep artifacts:** a shared note with decisions and action items; reviewing last month's note takes two minutes and proves the system works.
- **Cap the scope:** if a talk needs more than 30 minutes, schedule a separate working session — the monthly slot stays light and therefore keeps happening.

## FAQ

### We earn different amounts — does the structure change?
Structure stays; allocations may split differently: joint accounts for shared goals and bills, personal accounts for autonomy, agreed contribution percentages rather than 50/50 absolutes. The money date is where those agreements get refreshed as incomes change.

### What if one partner avoids money entirely?
Start with fifteen minutes, wins only, and one small decision — avoidance usually masks shame or conflict history, not indifference to outcomes. Shared goals (a trip, security) are better on-ramps than ledgers. If avoidance persists around serious issues (debt hidden, compulsive spending), a fee-only financial counselor or therapist is the right escalation, not a harsher monthly ambush.

### How detailed should the review get?
Category-level, not transaction-level. The date is for direction and decisions; receipts live in whatever app handles tracking. Detailed forensics at the table reliably turn review into cross-examination.

### Should single people do money dates too?
Yes — the solo version builds the same muscle: scheduled attention, explicit decisions, documented progress. Solo budgeting drifts the same way relationships do when nobody schedules the review.
