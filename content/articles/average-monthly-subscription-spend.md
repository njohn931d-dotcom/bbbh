There is no single reliable figure for "average monthly subscription spend," because published averages depend on who was surveyed, which year, and whether they counted phone plans, insurance and streaming. The number that matters is yours — and you can measure it in about 20 minutes.

This page shows you how, and gives you benchmarks to sanity-check the result.

## How to measure your own number

1. **Open your last two months of bank and card statements** (two, so you catch annual renewals and quarterly charges).
2. **Search for recurring merchants.** Look for the same name appearing in both months.
3. **List every recurring charge**, including annual ones divided by 12.
4. **Separate essentials from discretionary.** Phone, internet and insurance are not in the same category as a streaming service you have not opened in six weeks.
5. **Total each group.**

| Category | Examples | Count | Monthly total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Entertainment | Streaming, music, gaming | | |
| Software and cloud | Storage, productivity, AI tools | | |
| Health and fitness | Gym, apps, classes | | |
| Essentials | Phone, internet, insurance | | |
| Annual renewals | Divided by 12 | | |
| **Total** | | | |

## Annual charges are the ones people miss

A $120 annual renewal is $10 a month, but it never appears on a monthly list. Scan 12 months of statements for charges that appear once:

| Annual charge | Monthly equivalent | Work hours/year at $25/h |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $49 | $4.08 | 2.0 |
| $99 | $8.25 | 4.0 |
| $139 | $11.58 | 5.6 |
| $250 | $20.83 | 10.0 |

## Sanity-checking the total

Once you have your monthly figure, three checks tell you whether it is reasonable for *you*:

| Check | Calculation | Rough benchmark |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Share of take-home | monthly subscriptions ÷ monthly take-home | Under 5% is modest; over 10% is worth a review |
| Work hours | monthly total ÷ take-home hourly rate | Compare to how much you actually use |
| Cost per use | monthly cost ÷ times used | Under $1 per use is usually easy to justify |

| $60 | 1.8% | 2.4 | $720 |
| $100 | 3.0% | 4.0 | $1,200 |
| $180 | 5.4% | 7.2 | $2,160 |

## Why your number is probably higher than you think

Three effects inflate the gap between what people guess and what they actually pay:

- **Trial pricing expires.** A $4.99 introductory month becomes $15.99 in month four.
- **Price rises compound.** Three services each rising $2 a month is $72 a year with no new subscription added.
- **Duplication.** Two music services, two cloud storage plans, or a gym membership and a fitness app doing the same job.

## What to do with the number

If the total surprises you, the fastest wins are usually duplication and unused services rather than cancelling things you love. Work through [How to Audit Your Subscriptions in 30 Minutes](/articles/subscriptions/how-to-audit-your-subscriptions/), then decide whether each survivor is worth its [annual cost](/calculators/subscription-cost/) rather than its monthly one.

Assumptions: take-home pay, 12 equal months, no currency effects. Survey-based industry averages are deliberately not quoted here; they vary by methodology and date quickly.
