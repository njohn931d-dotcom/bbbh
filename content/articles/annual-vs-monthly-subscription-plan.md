Annual billing saves money when the discount is real and you would have kept the service for the full 12 months anyway. The test is simple: **compare the annual price with monthly price × 12, then ask how likely you are to still be using it in month nine.**

## The break-even test

```
Saving = (monthly price × 12) − annual price
Break-even month = annual price ÷ monthly price
```

The break-even month is the point where you start winning. If an annual plan costs the same as 10 monthly payments, you are worse off in months 1 to 10 and better off in 11 and 12 — and you lose money overall if you cancel in month six.

## What different discounts are worth

At a $15 monthly price:

| Annual price | Equivalent monthly | Discount | Annual saving | Work hours saved at $25/h |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $180 | $15.00 | 0% | $0 | 0 |
| $168 | $14.00 | 7% | $12 | 0.5 |
| $150 | $12.50 | 17% | $30 | 1.2 |
| $144 | $12.00 | 20% | $36 | 1.4 |
| $120 | $10.00 | 33% | $60 | 2.4 |
| $108 | $9.00 | 40% | $72 | 2.9 |

A 17% annual discount ($30 on a $180 service) is about 1.2 hours of work at a $25 take-home rate. That is real money, but it is also the scale of the decision: these are small savings, and they are only worth taking on services you are certain about.

## What you give up by paying annually

| Factor | Monthly | Annual |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cash flow | Smaller outgoings | One large payment |
| Cancellation | Stop any month | Often no refund |
| Price-rise protection | Rise hits within weeks | Locked for 12 months |
| Service risk | Low exposure | Full year at risk if it declines or shuts down |
| Discount | Full price | Usually 10–33% off |

## Four cases where monthly still wins

1. **You have not used it for a full year yet.** New services fail the "would I re-buy this" test surprisingly often.
2. **The service is changing.** A pending redesign, catalogue change or ownership change is a reason to stay flexible.
3. **The annual discount is under 10%.** Two or three weeks of flexibility is worth more than a $12 saving.
4. **Cash flow is tight.** A $180 annual charge in a month with an unexpected bill is worse than $15 twelve times.

## Two cases where annual clearly wins

1. **You have used it weekly for over a year and the discount is above 20%.**
2. **A price rise is announced.** Locking in the old rate for 12 months converts an increase into a delay — see [What to Do When a Subscription Raises Its Price](/articles/subscriptions/subscription-price-increase-what-to-do/).

## The hidden cost of annual plans nobody mentions

Annual renewals are the hardest charges to notice, because they appear once. A $168 renewal you forgot about is $168 you did not budget for. Two habits fix it:

- **Add a reminder 7 days before each renewal date.**
- **Keep one list of annual renewal dates.** Four or five annual subscriptions spread across the year are invisible on a monthly statement.
