Your real hourly wage is your take-home pay divided by every hour the job costs you — not just the hours on your contract. **Real hourly wage = (take-home pay − work costs) ÷ total hours given to work.**

A $60,000 salary at 40 contracted hours is $28.85 an hour gross. Add a 45-minute daily commute, 30 minutes of getting ready and five hours of unpaid overtime a week, and the same job is closer to $19 an hour.

## The formula, step by step

1. **Annual take-home pay** (after tax and deductions)
2. **Minus work costs**: commuting, parking, work clothes, childcare, meals bought at work
3. **Equals net pay for the job**
4. **Divided by total hours**: contracted hours + commute + preparation + unpaid overtime + work admin at home

## What the hidden hours do to the rate

Take-home pay of $43,200 a year, 2,080 contracted hours:

| Extra hours per week | Total hours/year | Real hourly wage | Compared to $20.77 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 2,080 | $20.77 | — |
| 3 | 2,236 | $19.32 | −7% |
| 5 | 2,340 | $18.46 | −11% |
| 7.5 | 2,470 | $17.49 | −16% |
| 10 | 2,600 | $16.62 | −20% |
| 15 | 2,860 | $15.10 | −27% |

A 45-minute commute each way plus 30 minutes of preparation is 10 hours a week — enough to cut a $20.77 hourly rate to $16.62.

## Then subtract the costs of working

| Cost | Typical monthly | Annual | Hourly equivalent at 2,340 h |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Commuting (fuel, transit, parking) | $120 – $400 | $1,440 – $4,800 | $0.62 – $2.05 |
| Work clothes and laundry | $20 – $80 | $240 – $960 | $0.10 – $0.41 |
| Food bought at work | $80 – $300 | $960 – $3,600 | $0.41 – $1.54 |
| Childcare during work hours | $300 – $1,500 | $3,600 – $18,000 | $1.54 – $7.69 |
| Professional fees and licences | $10 – $60 | $120 – $720 | $0.05 – $0.31 |

At the top of those ranges, childcare alone can remove more per hour than a $3 raise adds.

## Worked example

| Item | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Take-home pay | $43,200 |
| Contracted hours (40 × 52) | 2,080 |
| Commute (45 min each way) | +390 hours |
| Preparation (20 min/day) | +87 hours |
| Unpaid overtime (1 h/week) | +52 hours |
| Weekend email (1 h/week) | +52 hours |
| **Total hours** | **2,661** |
| Work costs (commute, food, clothes) | −$3,600 |
| **Net pay for the job** | **$39,600** |
| **Real hourly wage** | **$14.88** |

The advertised rate was $28.85 gross. The real rate is $14.88 — and that is the figure to use when deciding whether a purchase is worth the hours.

## Why this number changes decisions

| Decision | Hourly maths |
| --- | --- |
| Paying $40 for a cleaner | Worth it if your real rate is above $40 ÷ hours saved |
| A job with a longer commute | A 30-minute longer commute (each way) is 260 hours a year |
| Working overtime | Unpaid overtime lowers your hourly rate every time |
| Working from home | Recovers commute and preparation hours: often 300–500 hours a year |
| A $6 delivery fee | If it saves 45 minutes, compare it to your real hourly rate |

## The break-even commute test

```
Commute cost per year = (round-trip minutes ÷ 60) × workdays × hourly value
                        + transport costs
```

| One-way commute | Hours/year (240 days) | Value at $20/h | Plus $2,400 transport | Total annual cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 15 min | 120 | $2,400 | $2,400 | $4,800 |
| 30 min | 240 | $4,800 | $2,400 | $7,200 |
| 45 min | 360 | $7,200 | $2,400 | $9,600 |
| 60 min | 480 | $9,600 | $2,400 | $12,000 |

Valuing commute time at your real hourly rate is the honest way to compare two jobs with different salaries and different distances — and it often flips the answer.
