A sinking fund is money saved gradually for an expense you know is coming. Instead of absorbing a $900 car insurance bill in one month, you save $75 every month for a year and the bill is already paid when it arrives.

The formula: **monthly sinking fund contribution = expected annual cost ÷ 12** (or ÷ the number of months until it is due).

## Annual costs that sinking funds absorb

| Category | Typical annual cost | Monthly contribution | Work hours/year at $25/h |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Car insurance and registration | $900 – $2,400 | $75 – $200 | 36 – 96 |
| Car maintenance and tyres | $600 – $1,500 | $50 – $125 | 24 – 60 |
| Home or renters insurance | $200 – $1,200 | $17 – $100 | 8 – 48 |
| Gifts (birthdays, holidays) | $400 – $1,500 | $33 – $125 | 16 – 60 |
| Annual subscriptions | $100 – $600 | $8 – $50 | 4 – 24 |
| Medical and dental | $300 – $2,000 | $25 – $167 | 12 – 80 |
| Holidays and travel | $1,000 – $6,000 | $83 – $500 | 40 – 240 |
| Appliance replacement | $300 – $1,200 | $25 – $100 | 12 – 48 |
| Professional fees and licences | $200 – $1,500 | $17 – $125 | 8 – 60 |

Add your own categories. Most households discover four to eight of these, totalling $250–$900 a month — money that is genuinely committed, just not monthly.

## A worked example

| Category | Annual cost | Monthly | Months of runaway |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Car insurance | $1,080 | $90 | 12 |
| Car service and tyres | $720 | $60 | 12 |
| Holiday | $2,400 | $200 | 12 |
| Gifts | $600 | $50 | 12 |
| Appliance fund | $360 | $30 | 12 |
| **Total** | **$5,160** | **$430** | |

$430 a month looks like a lot until you notice it replaces five bills that would otherwise arrive unannounced — including a $2,400 holiday and a $1,080 insurance renewal in the same quarter.

## Starting a fund mid-year

If the bill is due in fewer than 12 months, divide by the months remaining:

| Cost | Months until due | Monthly needed | Extra if only 3 months left |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $1,200 | 12 | $100 | $400 |
| $1,200 | 6 | $200 | — |
| $1,200 | 3 | $400 | — |
| $2,400 | 9 | $267 | — |
| $2,400 | 4 | $600 | — |

This is why the first year of sinking funds feels expensive: you are catching up on categories that were always going to be paid, just not by you in advance.

## How to run them without a spreadsheet

**Option 1: one account, one note.** Keep all sinking funds in a single savings account and track the balance per category in a note. Simplest to maintain; requires discipline not to double-count.

**Option 2: separate pots.** Many banks offer sub-accounts or "spaces." One per category, each with its own standing order. More setup, far less thinking.

**Option 3: an annual calendar.** List every irregular bill against the month it lands, then set the monthly transfer so the balance peaks just before.

| Month | Bill expected | Amount | Fund balance needed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| January | Insurance renewal | $1,080 | $1,080 |
| March | Car service | $320 | $320 |
| July | Holiday | $2,400 | $2,400 |
| November | Registration | $240 | $240 |
| December | Gifts | $600 | $600 |

## Sinking funds vs an emergency fund

| | Sinking fund | Emergency fund |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Purpose | Known, expected costs | Unknown, unexpected costs |
| You know the amount | Yes | No |
| You know the date | Usually | No |
| Typical target | The cost itself | Several months of essentials |
| Spending it | Planned | Last resort |

They are not interchangeable. A sinking fund for a known bill protects the emergency fund from being raided for predictable costs — see [How Big Should My Emergency Fund Be?](/articles/saving-habits/how-big-should-my-emergency-fund-be/).

## The two rules

1. **Contribute every month, whatever the balance.** A fund you skip in a tight month is a bill you will pay twice for later.
2. **Only spend it on the category.** The reason sinking funds work is the boundary. Breaking it once for "just this month" turns the whole system into one blurred savings pot.

Assumptions: no interest, investment returns or inflation. Cost ranges are illustrative; replace them with your own bills. General information, not financial advice.
