A sinking fund is money set aside monthly for a known future expense — car insurance due in November, the December holidays, a laptop replacement — so the bill arrives pre-funded instead of ambushing your checking account. It's the difference between a non-event and a "how are we going to cover this" week.

## Sinking fund vs. emergency fund

They solve different problems. An **emergency fund** covers the *unknown*: job loss, medical urgent, the pipe that burst. A **sinking fund** covers the *known but irregular*: expenses you can already put on next year's calendar. People raid emergency funds constantly not because emergencies multiply, but because predictable costs were never funded separately — a car service is not an emergency; it's a Tuesday that happens once a year. Keeping them in separate accounts (or visibly separate buckets) protects both functions.

## How to build one

1. **List the irregular expenses for the next 12 months.** Scan last year's statements — that's where the "surprises" hide: annual insurance premiums, registration, holidays and gifts, travel, back-to-school, medical deductibles, clothing seasons, home maintenance, tech replacements.
2. **Total each category annually,** then divide by 12 (or by the number of paychecks until the due date).
3. **Open the bucket.** Separate savings accounts, named sub-savings at most banks, or one "sinking" account with a spreadsheet ledger — all work. Separate named buckets beat one pooled account because the purpose survives contact with spending.
4. **Automate the contribution** on payday, alongside your pay-yourself-first transfer.
5. **Spend from the fund guilt-free** when the date arrives — that's the fund succeeding, not failing. Replenish the schedule the same month.

### Worked example

| Expense | Annual cost | Monthly set-aside |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Car insurance | $1,200 | $100 |
| Holidays & gifts | $900 | $75 |
| Car maintenance | $600 | $50 |
| Winter clothing | $400 | $33 |
| Laptop replacement ($1,400 in 3 years) | ~$470 | $39 |
| **Total** | **$3,570** | **$297** |

$297 a month converts $3,570 of "surprises" into scheduled non-events. Adjust the table to your life — the structure is the point.

## Choosing the right bucket granularity

Too few buckets and money gets fungible ("what's in the vacation account — $400 or $1,800?"). Too many and the transfers drown you. A workable middle: 4–7 categories max — predictable bills, vehicle, home, travel, gifts/holidays, replacements — with a catch-all "annual misc" absorbing the rest. Rebalance bucket totals at your monthly money check-in.

## Where to park the money

The money is short-term and dated, so it wants liquidity: a normal savings account or a high-yield account with same-week access. No investing for funds due within ~12 months — a market dip the month before your insurance renewal turns a boring bill into a real problem. Money due more than 18 months out can live in conservative investments if your risk tolerance allows; everything nearer stays cash-like.

## FAQ

### How is a sinking fund different from a budget category?
A budget category covers this month's spending; a sinking fund funds an expense whose month hasn't arrived yet. It's a budget line that spans the year — the monthly transfer is the budget, the balance is the pre-paid bill.

### What if I don't know the exact future cost?
Estimate from last year's actuals plus a margin (10–15% buffer for inflation-sensitive items like car work). Estimates you fund beat perfect numbers you don't — underfunded buckets get topped up from the catch-all category.

### Should I keep sinking funds in the same account as my emergency fund?
Physically separate or clearly bucketed is better — mixing them makes emergency withdrawals indistinguishable from planned spending, and the emergency fund quietly thins. Most banks allow multiple named savings accounts for free.

### Can I use a checking account with envelopes instead?
Yes — digital envelopes named per sinking category achieve the same thing (the envelope method works fine for future-dated expenses). Choose whichever system you'll actually check on payday.
