The monthly payment is the smallest interesting number about a car. The real figure includes depreciation, insurance, fuel or charging, maintenance, tyres, registration and parking — and for most cars, those extras add up to more than the finance payment.

## The six cost blocks

| Block | What it includes | Usually monthly or annual |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Depreciation | Loss in value while you own it | Annual (invisible until you sell) |
| Finance or lease | Payments and interest | Monthly |
| Insurance | Premium, plus any excess | Monthly or annual |
| Energy | Fuel or electricity | Monthly |
| Maintenance and tyres | Servicing, repairs, consumables | Annual, lumpy |
| Fixed costs | Registration, parking, tolls, cleaning | Annual or monthly |

## Worked example: a $28,000 car, five years

| Item | Monthly | Annual | Five-year total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Finance payment ($450/mo, 60 mo) | $450 | $5,400 | $27,000 |
| Depreciation (to $11,000) | $283 | $3,400 | $17,000 |
| Insurance | $150 | $1,800 | $9,000 |
| Fuel (12,000 miles/yr at 32 mpg, $3.30/gal) | $104 | $1,238 | $6,188 |
| Maintenance and repairs | $85 | $1,020 | $5,100 |
| Tyres | $20 | $240 | $1,200 |
| Registration and inspection | $25 | $300 | $1,500 |
| Parking and tolls | $60 | $720 | $3,600 |
| **Total** | **$1,177** | **$14,118** | **$70,588** |

The finance payment is 38% of the real cost. Depreciation — which never appears on a statement — is the largest single line after it.

## Cost per mile and per minute

```
Cost per mile = annual running cost ÷ annual miles
Cost per trip = cost per mile × miles per trip
```

| Annual running cost | 6,000 miles | 10,000 miles | 15,000 miles | 20,000 miles |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $6,000 | $1.00/mi | $0.60/mi | $0.40/mi | $0.30/mi |
| $9,000 | $1.50/mi | $0.90/mi | $0.60/mi | $0.45/mi |
| $12,000 | $2.00/mi | $1.20/mi | $0.80/mi | $0.60/mi |
| $14,118 | $2.35/mi | $1.41/mi | $0.94/mi | $0.71/mi |

At $0.94 a mile, a 12-mile round trip to work costs $11.28 before parking. Compare that with a transit fare or the same trip by bike before assuming the car is cheaper.

## In work hours

At a $25 take-home hourly rate:

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $600 | 24 | 3.0 | 288 |
| $900 | 36 | 4.5 | 432 |
| $1,177 | 47 | 5.9 | 565 |

A $1,177 monthly running cost is nearly six working days a month — roughly a quarter of a full-time working life spent funding one car.

## What changes the total most

| Lever | Typical effect | Effort |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Buying a cheaper car | Depreciation scales with price — often the biggest single saving | One decision |
| Buying used, 2–4 years old | Avoids the steepest depreciation years | One decision |
| Keeping it longer | Spreads depreciation and purchase costs over more years | Low |
| Fewer miles | Reduces fuel, maintenance, tyres and depreciation | Ongoing |
| Shopping insurance annually | Renewal quotes are frequently higher than new-customer quotes | 30 minutes a year |
| Fuel efficiency or EV | Changes the energy line substantially | Depends on mileage |

Depreciation is usually the largest lever and the least visible, because it is only realised when you sell. Keeping a car two years longer is often worth more than any negotiation on the purchase price.

## New vs used, modelled

| | New $32,000 | Used 3-year-old $20,000 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Purchase price | $32,000 | $20,000 |
| Value after 5 years | $13,500 | $8,000 |
| Depreciation | $18,500 | $12,000 |
| Maintenance (5 years) | $4,000 | $6,500 |
| Finance interest (60 mo, 6%) | ~$5,100 | ~$3,200 |
| **Five-year total (excl. fuel, insurance)** | **$27,600** | **$21,700** |

Roughly $5,900 in favour of the used car over five years — about 236 hours of work at $25 an hour. Terms, interest rates and local markets vary; run your own numbers with the quotes you actually have.

## Build your own model

1. Take your finance or lease payment.
2. Add your insurance premium (annual ÷ 12).
3. Add fuel or charging from three months of receipts.
4. Add $85–$120 a month for maintenance and tyres if you do not track it.
5. Add registration, parking, tolls and cleaning.
6. Add depreciation: (purchase price − expected resale) ÷ months you will keep it.
7. Divide by your monthly mileage for cost per mile.

Then put the monthly total into the [cost-of-time calculator](/calculators/cost-of-time/) to see it in hours of work, and compare the payment alone in [How Many Hours of Work Does a Car Payment Cost?](/articles/work-hours/how-many-hours-of-work-does-a-car-payment-cost/).

Assumptions: illustrative figures — depreciation, insurance, fuel prices, interest rates and maintenance vary enormously by car, location, driver and year. Replace every number with your own quotes before deciding.
