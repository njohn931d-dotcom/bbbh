A $3,500 holiday costs **140 hours of work** at a $25 take-home hourly rate — three and a half working weeks. Put another way, each day of a week-long trip is worth a full working day of your time.

Vacations are usually budgeted in money and measured in memories. Adding a third unit — hours of work — makes the trade-off easier to judge.

## Holiday budget in work hours and weeks

At a $25 take-home hourly rate (a 40-hour week):

| Trip budget | Work hours | 40-hour work weeks | Cost per day (7 days) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $1,000 | 40 | 1.0 | $142.86 |
| $2,000 | 80 | 2.0 | $285.71 |
| $3,500 | 140 | 3.5 | $500.00 |
| $5,000 | 200 | 5.0 | $714.29 |
| $8,000 | 320 | 8.0 | $1,142.86 |
| $12,000 | 480 | 12.0 | $1,714.29 |

## The cost people forget: unpaid days

Paid leave is part of your compensation. Unpaid leave is not. If a seven-day trip uses five working days and two of them are unpaid, the real cost is higher than the invoice:

```
Real cost = trip spend + (unpaid days × daily take-home pay)
```

| Daily take-home pay | 2 unpaid days | 5 unpaid days | 10 unpaid days |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $120 | $240 | $600 | $1,200 |
| $200 | $400 | $1,000 | $2,000 |
| $320 | $640 | $1,600 | $3,200 |
| $500 | $1,000 | $2,500 | $5,000 |

Freelancers and contractors rarely have paid leave at all, which is why a $3,500 trip can cost closer to $5,000 in real terms.

## Cost per day is the number worth optimising

Two trips with the same budget are not equal if one lasts four days and the other lasts twelve. Cost per day (total ÷ nights away) tells you which one is doing more work for your money. Extending a trip by three nights often adds accommodation and food but no extra flights — which is why the third week of a long trip is frequently the cheapest per day.

## Where the money actually goes

A trip budget usually splits roughly into four buckets: transport, accommodation, food, and everything else (activities, visas, insurance, souvenirs). Writing your own estimate into those four lines, then converting each to hours of work, shows which bucket is worth trimming. Cutting $500 of transport is 20 hours of work; cutting $500 of activities may cost you the reason you went.
