A $2 monthly rise sounds trivial and costs $24 a year. Across six services, that is $144 a year for nothing new — which is why price rises, not new subscriptions, are usually what inflate a monthly bill over time.

When a rise lands, you have five options, in this order.

## The five responses, in order

**1. Check the notice period.** Providers generally must tell you before a rise takes effect, and the notice often gives you a window to cancel without penalty. The email is the document that matters — find it before you decide.

**2. Ask for the old price.** Retention offers are routine. One message — "I am considering cancelling at the new price; is the previous rate available?" — works often enough to be worth two minutes, especially on annual or long-tenure accounts.

**3. Downgrade a tier.** The rise frequently applies to premium tiers only. Moving from a $22.99 premium plan to a $15.49 standard plan can cost you less than you paid before the rise.

**4. Switch to annual billing.** Annual plans are usually billed at roughly ten monthly payments and lock the rate for 12 months. Check the break-even month first — see [Annual vs Monthly Subscription Plans](/articles/subscriptions/annual-vs-monthly-subscription-plan/).

**5. Leave.** If the new price exceeds what you would pay if you were signing up today, that is the answer. Cancel, note the renewal date, and set a reminder to re-evaluate in three months.

## The only question that matters

> Would you sign up today, at the new price, if you had never used the service?

If yes, pay it. If no — but you would sign up at the old price — downgrade, ask for retention, or go annual. If no at any price, cancel.

Individually these are rounding errors. Together, eight services rising $5 each is $480 a year — roughly three weeks of work at a $25 take-home rate. This is the strongest argument for the twice-yearly audit in [How to Audit Your Subscriptions](/articles/subscriptions/how-to-audit-your-subscriptions/).

## Before you accept the new price

- Check whether the rise applies to your tier or only to new subscribers.
- Check whether an ad-supported tier now exists at your old price.
- Check whether a competitor's price is lower — and whether the switching cost (losing playlists, history, downloads) is worth it to you.
- Check whether you are paying for a bundle you no longer use in full.

Assumptions: prices constant after the rise, no taxes or currency effects, no promotional rates. Notice requirements and refund rights vary by provider and country.
