A $450 monthly car payment costs **18 hours of work a month** at a $25 take-home hourly rate — about $5,400 and 216 work hours a year. That is more than five 40-hour weeks of work, every year, for the length of the loan.

## The total you actually pay

A car loan is a commitment, not a monthly price. Multiply the payment by the number of months:

| Monthly payment | 36 months | 48 months | 60 months | 72 months |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $350 | $12,600 | $16,800 | $21,000 | $25,200 |
| $450 | $16,200 | $21,600 | $27,000 | $32,400 |
| $600 | $21,600 | $28,800 | $36,000 | $43,200 |
| $800 | $28,800 | $38,400 | $48,000 | $57,600 |

Stretching a $450 payment from 36 to 72 months adds $16,200 — 648 hours of work at $25 an hour. The interest portion is the difference between what you pay and the amount borrowed, so a longer term with the same payment usually means a smaller car or a bigger total bill.

## What share of your pay it takes

| Monthly take-home | $300 payment | $450 payment | $700 payment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $2,500 | 12% | 18% | 28% |
| $3,333 | 9% | 13.5% | 21% |
| $4,167 | 7.2% | 10.8% | 16.8% |
| $6,000 | 5% | 7.5% | 11.7% |

A commonly cited guideline is to keep total vehicle costs — payment, insurance, fuel, maintenance — to roughly 15–20% of take-home pay. It is a rule of thumb, not a rule: in areas without public transport, a car is not optional, and in cities with good transit it may be.

## The payment is only part of the cost

The monthly figure you signed for excludes insurance, fuel or charging, maintenance, tyres, registration and parking. See [The True Cost of Car Ownership](/articles/spending-decisions/true-cost-of-car-ownership/) for a full model — the payment is often half or less of what a car actually costs to run.

## Three questions before signing

1. **What is the total, not the monthly?** Payment × months, plus interest.
2. **How many hours of work is that per month?** Payment ÷ take-home hourly rate.
3. **What happens in month 37?** A loan that ends before the car does is a very different proposition from one that overlaps with your next loan.

Assumptions: take-home hourly pay, 12 equal payments a year, no balloon payment, no changes in insurance or fuel costs. Interest, taxes, registration and depreciation are not included.
