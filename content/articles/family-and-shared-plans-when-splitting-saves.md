A family plan saves money when the shared price divided by the number of people is lower than your individual price, and everyone actually uses it. **Cost per person = family plan price ÷ number of users.**

## Break-even by number of people

With an individual plan at $11.99 a month:

| Family plan price | 2 people | 3 people | 4 people | 5 people | 6 people |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $16.99 | $8.50 | $5.66 | $4.25 | $3.40 | $2.83 |
| $19.99 | $10.00 | $6.66 | $5.00 | $4.00 | $3.33 |
| $24.99 | $12.50 | $8.33 | $6.25 | $5.00 | $4.17 |
| $29.99 | $15.00 | $10.00 | $7.50 | $6.00 | $5.00 |

At $19.99 shared four ways, each person pays $5.00 instead of $11.99 — a saving of $6.99 a month, $83.88 a year, about 3.4 hours of work at a $25 take-home rate. With two people the same plan costs $10.00 each: a saving, but a much smaller one.

## What the table does not show

Shared plans have costs that never appear on the invoice:

| Hidden cost | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Household or region restrictions | Many plans require members to live at the same address; enforcement varies by provider |
| One payer, one risk | The account owner is liable for the whole amount if someone stops paying |
| Profile and recommendation mixing | Shared algorithms; downloads and history become communal |
| Seat limits | Adding a fifth or sixth person sometimes means a second plan |
| Cancellation friction | Leaving a shared plan can lock out everyone's libraries or files |

Check the provider's own household rules before splitting a plan with people outside your home. Some providers permit it explicitly; others treat it as a terms-of-service breach and can suspend the account.

## When a shared plan is not worth it

1. **You are the only one who uses it.** Paying $24.99 for a plan you share with nobody is worse than $11.99.
2. **The split is unequal.** If two of four people use 90% of it, an individual plan for the light users plus a duo plan for the heavy users can be cheaper.
3. **You would not pay the individual price anyway.** Sharing a $30 plan five ways is only a saving if each person would have paid $12 on their own.
4. **You need features the shared tier drops.** Some family plans remove offline downloads, higher quality or concurrent streams at the cheapest tier.

## Rules that prevent arguments

- **One owner, one payment method, one date.** Write down who pays, who owes what, and when.
- **Agree an exit.** Anyone can leave at a renewal date with 30 days' notice; nobody leaves mid-period leaving others short.
- **Automate the repayments.** A standing order for each person's share removes the monthly reminder.
- **Review at renewal.** Usage changes; so should the split.

## Splitting costs beyond streaming

| Type | Usually shareable | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Music and video | Yes, with household limits | Check concurrent-stream limits |
| Cloud storage | Yes, with per-user quotas | Each member often gets a separate private allocation |
| Password managers | Yes | Often the best value of any shared plan |
| Productivity suites | Yes | Verify licence terms for business use |
| VPN and security | Sometimes | Household or device limits vary |
| Single-seat software licences | Usually no | Check the licence before sharing |

## Run the numbers for your household

Add up what everyone currently pays individually, then divide the shared plan price by the number of real users. The [subscription calculator](/calculators/subscription-cost/) converts whichever plan you choose into an annual figure and hours of work.

Assumptions: equal splits, prices constant, no taxes or currency effects. Provider terms on household eligibility vary; read them before splitting a plan.
