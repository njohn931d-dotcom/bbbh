The standard full-time working year is **2,080 hours**: 40 hours a week × 52 weeks. It is the divisor behind almost every hourly-to-salary conversion, including the ones on this site.

It is also an approximation that assumes no unpaid leave, no holidays and no sick days — which is why the number you should use may be lower.

## Where 2,080 comes from

| Step | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Hours per week | 40 |
| Weeks per year | 52 |
| Hours per year | 2,080 |
| Weekdays in 2026 | 261 |
| Weekdays minus 8 public holidays | 253 |
| Working hours at 253 days × 8 h | 2,024 |

2026 has 261 weekdays. With eight public holidays and no vacation, that is 2,024 hours — 56 hours short of 2,080.

## What leave does to the total

Starting from 2,080 hours (40 h × 52 weeks):

| Deduction | Hours removed | Working hours left |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 8 public holidays | 64 | 2,016 |
| + 2 weeks vacation | 80 | 1,936 |
| + 3 weeks vacation | 120 | 1,896 |
| + 4 weeks vacation | 160 | 1,856 |
| + 5 sick days | 40 | 1,816 |
| + 2 weeks unpaid leave | 80 | 1,736 |

Paid leave does not change your *pay*; it changes how many hours you work for that pay — which raises your effective hourly rate. Unpaid leave changes both.

## Which divisor should you use?

| Situation | Divisor for annual pay | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Standard full-time, paid holidays | 2,080 | The convention |
| Full-time with 4 weeks paid leave | 1,920 | More accurate effective rate |
| Full-time with 2 weeks unpaid | 2,000 | 50 working weeks |
| 35-hour week | 1,820 | Adjusts to contract |
| Part-time, 25 h/week | 1,300 | |
| Freelance | Billable days × hours | See [day rate guide](/articles/pay-and-rates/how-to-set-a-freelance-day-rate/) |

## Monthly and weekly divisors

| Period | Hours at 40 h/week | Use for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Per week | 40 | Weekly pay |
| Per two weeks | 80 | Biweekly pay |
| Per month | 173.33 | 2,080 ÷ 12 |
| Per quarter | 520 | Quarterly figures |
| Per year | 2,080 | Annual salary |

The monthly figure of 173.33 is an average — real months range from 160 to 184 working hours. Salaries paid monthly are usually 1/12 of annual pay regardless of hours in the month, which is why some months feel better value per hour than others.

## Leap years and the extra day

A leap year adds one day (366 instead of 365). Depending on which day of the week it falls, that is either an extra working day or an extra weekend day for most schedules, and it can create the occasional extra pay period on a biweekly payroll. Over a working life the effect is small: roughly 8 extra hours every four years.

## Why the number matters for every calculation on this site

Every price-to-hours conversion divides by your hourly pay, and your hourly pay is annual pay ÷ hours. Using 2,080 when you really work 1,920 understates your hourly rate by about 8% — which makes every purchase look slightly more expensive than it is. Using gross instead of take-home pay does the opposite, and by more.
