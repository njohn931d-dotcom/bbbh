Saving $5 a day for a year puts **$1,825** in the bank. On workdays only (about 260), the same challenge totals $1,300. No interest, no investment returns — just a daily amount multiplied by the number of days you keep it up.

## Daily amount to yearly total

| Daily amount | 30 days | 90 days | 180 days | 365 days |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $1 | $30 | $90 | $180 | $365 |
| $2 | $60 | $180 | $360 | $730 |
| $3 | $90 | $270 | $540 | $1,095 |
| $5 | $150 | $450 | $900 | $1,825 |
| $7.50 | $225 | $675 | $1,350 | $2,737.50 |
| $10 | $300 | $900 | $1,800 | $3,650 |
| $20 | $600 | $1,800 | $3,600 | $7,300 |

## If you only save on weekdays

| Daily amount | 5 days/week | 20 days/month | 260 days/year |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $3 | $15 | $60 | $780 |
| $5 | $25 | $100 | $1,300 |
| $10 | $50 | $200 | $2,600 |
| $15 | $75 | $300 | $3,900 |

Skipping weekends costs $525 a year at $5 a day — $1,825 becomes $1,300. Both are real savings; pick the schedule you will actually follow rather than the one that looks best in a table.

## Three ways to run the challenge

**1. Transfer it.** Move the amount to a separate savings account the same day. Automation beats discipline: a standing order on payday for a week's worth ($35) removes 365 decisions.

**2. Skip the purchase.** Give up one daily buy — coffee, a snack, a delivery fee — and move that money instead. The saving only counts if it does not reappear elsewhere in your spending.

**3. Round up and top up.** Round every card purchase up to the nearest dollar and move the difference, adding a fixed daily amount.

## Why daily challenges fail (and the fix)

| Failure mode | Fix |
| --- | --- |
| Forgetting individual days | Automate a weekly or monthly transfer instead |
| The amount is too big by week three | Start at $1–$2; raise it after a month |
| Saving then spending it back | Use a separate account, ideally one without a card |
| All-or-nothing thinking on a missed day | Missed days are not failures; double up or carry on |
| No visible progress | Keep a running total you can see — the streak is the motivator |

## A 90-day starter version

| Weeks | Daily amount | Weekly transfer | Total at 90 days |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1–4 | $2 | $14 | $56 (after 4 weeks) |
| 5–8 | $4 | $28 | $168 (cumulative by week 8) |
| 9–13 | $6 | $42 | $378 (cumulative by week 13) |

Ramping the amount is easier than starting at $5, and it gets you to $378 in 90 days rather than quitting in week two.
