A big purchase is any purchase you would notice losing. Seven questions, answered honestly, resolve most of them — and they take about ten minutes.

## 1. How many hours of work is this?

```
Hours = price ÷ take-home hourly rate
```

| Price | At $15/h | $25/h | $40/h | $75/h |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $500 | 33.3 | 20 | 12.5 | 6.7 |
| $1,000 | 66.7 | 40 | 25 | 13.3 |
| $2,500 | 166.7 | 100 | 62.5 | 33.3 |
| $5,000 | 333.3 | 200 | 125 | 66.7 |

## 2. What is the total cost of ownership?

Add everything the purchase commits you to:

| Cost type | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| Purchase price | Including tax and delivery |
| Running costs | Energy, fuel, consumables, servicing |
| Recurring costs | Subscriptions, insurance, licences |
| Accessories | The things it needs to be usable |
| End costs | Disposal, resale fees, early-termination |

A $1,200 laptop with $60 of software a year and a $90 replacement battery in year three is $1,470 over three years.

## 3. What is the cost per use, or per year?

```
Cost per use = total cost of ownership ÷ expected uses
Cost per year = total cost of ownership ÷ expected years
```

| Item | Price | Years | Cost/year | Uses/year | Cost per use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sofa | $1,800 | 12 | $150 | Daily | ~$0.41 |
| Espresso machine | $700 | 6 | $117 | 300 cups | $0.39 |
| Road bike | $1,500 | 8 | $188 | 60 rides | $3.13 |
| Second car | $9,000 | 6 | $1,500 | 200 trips | $7.50 |

See [Cost Per Use](/articles/spending-decisions/cost-per-use-how-to-compare-purchases/) for the full method.

## 4. What does this displace?

Money spent here is money not spent elsewhere. Name the alternative: three months of a hobby, a week's holiday, two months of the mortgage, or the emergency fund's next $1,000.

| Purchase | Equivalent to |
| --- | --- |
| $1,200 phone | 48 hours of work at $25/h, or a weekend away twice a year |
| $2,500 laptop | 2.5 months of a $1,000/mo rent, or 100 hours of work |
| $9,000 car | 6 months of a $1,500/mo housing cost, or 360 hours |

## 5. Would I buy it again at this price in a year?

The strongest single test. If the honest answer is no, the purchase is driven by novelty, urgency or pressure rather than by use value.

## 6. What is the return and repair window?

| Check | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Return window length | A 14-day window and a 90-day window produce very different decisions |
| Restocking fees | Can make a return uneconomic |
| Warranty length and terms | What is covered, and who pays shipping |
| Repairability and spare parts | Determines whether year four is a repair or a replacement |

## 7. Can I wait 30 days?

| Rule | Apply to | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 24 hours | Under a day's take-home pay | Cuts novelty buys |
| 7 days | Around a week's pay | Allows comparison shopping |
| 30 days | A month's pay or more | Removes urgency as a factor |

Waiting is free for most purchases and expensive for none — with the genuine exception of time-limited needs. A purchase that survives 30 days of thinking is usually one you will use.

## Worked example: a $1,400 camera

| Question | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| Hours of work | 56 h at $25/h take-home |
| Total cost of ownership | $1,400 + $250 lens + $80 bag + $60 insurance/yr = ~$1,970 over 3 years |
| Cost per use | 40 shoots a year × 3 = 120 → $16.42 |
| What it displaces | Two-thirds of a $3,000 holiday fund |
| Buy again in a year? | Yes — photography is an established hobby |
| Return window | 30 days, free returns |
| Wait 30 days? | Yes; price unchanged, decision confirmed |

Verdict: buy — because the cost per use is justified by an existing habit, not a hoped-for one.

## Use it as a checklist

Copy the seven questions into a note before your next large purchase. For the smaller ones, the [cost-of-time calculator](/calculators/cost-of-time/) answers question 1 in a few seconds, and the [24-hour rule guide](/guides/24-hour-rule/) covers question 7.

Assumptions: take-home pay, no financing costs, no inflation. Cost-per-use figures depend on honest estimates of frequency.
