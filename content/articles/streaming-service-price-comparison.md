Comparing streaming services on monthly price alone is misleading: an ad-free tier at $22.99 is not "more expensive" than a $7.99 ad-supported tier if you watch 40 hours a month on one and 3 hours on the other. **The useful metric is cost per hour watched.**

## Typical price tiers (check current prices before relying on them)

Streaming prices change frequently, and they vary by country, so treat these as structure rather than exact figures. Verify on the provider's own site:

| Tier | Typical monthly range | What it usually includes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ad-supported entry | $6 – $10 | Ads, HD, sometimes one stream |
| Standard ad-free | $15 – $19 | No ads, HD or 4K, two streams |
| Premium ad-free | $20 – $28 | 4K, four streams, downloads, spatial audio |
| Live TV bundle | $60 – $95 | Live channels plus on-demand |
| Annual plan | ~10 months of the monthly price | One payment, usually no refunds |

## The metric that decides it: cost per hour

```
Cost per hour watched = monthly price ÷ hours watched per month
```

| Monthly price | 2 h/month | 10 h/month | 25 h/month | 60 h/month |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $7.99 | $4.00/h | $0.80/h | $0.32/h | $0.13/h |
| $12.99 | $6.50/h | $1.30/h | $0.52/h | $0.22/h |
| $17.99 | $9.00/h | $1.80/h | $0.72/h | $0.30/h |
| $22.99 | $11.50/h | $2.30/h | $0.92/h | $0.38/h |

At 2 hours a month, even the cheapest tier costs $4.00 an hour — more than most cinemas charge per film. At 25 hours, the premium tier costs under a dollar an hour. **Usage, not price, decides whether a streaming plan is good value.**

## Worked example: three services, one household

| Service | Tier | Monthly | Hours watched | Cost/hour | Annual |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | Standard ad-free | $17.99 | 22 | $0.82 | $215.88 |
| B | Ad-supported | $7.99 | 3 | $2.66 | $95.88 |
| C | Premium | $22.99 | 40 | $0.57 | $275.88 |
| **Total** | | **$48.97** | **65** | **$0.75** | **$587.64** |

Service B is the cheapest and the worst value. Dropping it saves $95.88 a year — 3.8 hours of work at a $25 take-home rate — for the loss of 3 hours of viewing a month.

## Ad-supported vs ad-free: the actual trade-off

| | Ad-supported | Ad-free |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly cost | Lower | Higher |
| Time cost | Ads add minutes per hour | None |
| Catalogue | Sometimes reduced | Usually complete |
| Offline downloads | Sometimes excluded | Usually included |

If an ad tier saves $8 a month and ads cost you 6 minutes an hour, watching 30 hours a month costs 3 hours of ads to save $8 — worth it only if your hour is worth under $2.67. For most people, ad-free is the better arithmetic at high usage; for light viewers, the ad tier wins.

## Four ways to cut the bill without watching less

1. **Rotate instead of stack.** Keep one or two services at a time; subscribe to a third when its catalogue has something you want.
2. **Check for an annual plan.** Typically around two months free — see the break-even test in [Annual vs Monthly Subscription Plans](/articles/subscriptions/annual-vs-monthly-subscription-plan/).
3. **Use a shared or family plan.** See [Family and Shared Plans: When Splitting Saves](/articles/subscriptions/family-and-shared-plans-when-splitting-saves/).
4. **Audit twice a year.** A 30-minute review is the highest-return habit on this page: [How to Audit Your Subscriptions](/articles/subscriptions/how-to-audit-your-subscriptions/).
