15 Recurring Commission Affiliate Programs That Pay Monthly

RC By Riley Chen, Founder & Lead Researcher Updated 26 September 2026 10 min read

Quick answer: recurring commission affiliate programs pay you 20–40% of a subscription every month for as long as the customer stays. Refer 100 customers to a $99/month product at 30% and you earn about $2,970 every month from work you already finished — the closest thing affiliate marketing has to genuine passive income. The critical terms to check are how long the commission lasts (12 months vs lifetime is a 3–5× difference in value) and the clawback window.

How recurring commissions work

Instead of a single payment when someone buys, you receive a share of their subscription fee every month they stay. The commission is usually a percentage of the monthly or annual fee, tracked against your affiliate ID for the lifetime of that customer's account.

The mechanical difference from every other affiliate model is that your income from a given piece of content keeps growing after the content stops getting new visitors. A review page that converts 20 customers in month one pays you for 20 customers in month one, 20 in month two, and so on — plus whatever it converts in those months.

This is why recurring programs are the closest thing in affiliate marketing to an asset rather than a job. Everything else is linear: more traffic, more money. Recurring is cumulative.

Duration and clawbacks: read this before anything else

The headline commission rate is the least important number in a recurring program. These three terms determine what you actually get paid:

TermWhat to look forWhy it matters
DurationLifetime > 24 months > 12 months > 3 monthsA 30% lifetime deal is worth 3–5× a 30% deal that stops at 12 months. This is the biggest single variable in the entire model.
Clawback windowShorter is better; look for 30–60 daysIf a customer cancels or refunds inside the window, the commission is reversed. A 90-day clawback on a high-churn product can wipe out most of your earnings.
Recurring basisPercentage of the actual fee paidSome programs calculate on list price while the customer pays a discount, or exclude add-ons and usage fees. Check what the percentage applies to.
Attribution window60–90 days for considered purchasesSoftware trials mean the sale often happens weeks after the click. A 30-day cookie on a 14-day trial plus decision time loses credit.
Payment thresholdKnow the minimum before payoutSmall recurring amounts can sit below a payout threshold for months. Not a dealbreaker, but plan cash flow around it.

Modelling churn before you commit

Recurring income has a risk that one-off commissions do not: customers leave. A 40% recurring commission on a product that loses 15% of customers a month can pay less over two years than 20% on a product that keeps 97%.

How to estimate churn you cannot see: look at the vendor's public retention claims sceptically, check whether they publish customer counts over time, and prefer tools that businesses depend on operationally — accounting, CRM, hosting, infrastructure — over discretionary ones. Switching costs protect your recurring income more than commission rate does.

Content that converts subscriptions

Subscription buyers behave differently from retail shoppers. They are committing to an ongoing relationship, so their concerns are different: will this still fit in a year, what happens when I outgrow it, how painful is leaving.

  • “X vs Y” comparisons — the highest-intent page type for subscriptions, because the reader is choosing a platform to live in, not a product to own.
  • “X alternatives” — buyers leaving a competitor are the most valuable visitors you can get, and many SaaS programs run 60–90 day cookies so a slow switch still pays you.
  • Pricing-page explainers — subscription pricing is deliberately confusing. “What [Product] actually costs at 5, 20 and 100 seats” answers a question the vendor's own site avoids.
  • Migration guides — “[Competitor] to [Product]: what actually breaks” converts extraordinarily well and is almost impossible to write without real experience.
  • Use-case content — “How to set up [workflow] in [product]” attracts people mid-trial, which is the exact moment a subscription decision is made.

Stacking recurring with other models

Recurring commissions pair better with other affiliate models than any other combination, because they fill in the gaps in cash flow and coverage:

Recurring + retail

Amazon converts impulse buyers and tells you what your audience wants. Recurring programs pay the margin on the same traffic. One page, two links, very different economics.

Recurring + high ticket

High ticket pays for the months a big deal closes; recurring fills the gaps and compounds in the background. This is the most resilient combination in affiliate marketing. See the high ticket playbook.

Recurring + email list

Subscriptions have trials, migrations and renewal cycles. An email list lets you be present at each decision point instead of hoping the reader returns within a 90-day cookie window.

Recurring + free tool

A calculator or template captures the visitor, then recommends the subscription that solves the next step. This site's income calculator is that pattern in miniature.

FAQ

What is a recurring commission affiliate program?

A program that pays you a percentage of the customer's subscription every month for as long as they stay subscribed, rather than one payment at purchase. Typical rates are 20–40% of the monthly fee, with some programs paying for the lifetime of the account. Payments stack, so 100 customers at $29/month produces roughly $870 every month from work already completed.

Are recurring commissions better than a one-off payout?

Usually, but not instantly. A 20% recurring commission on a $99/month subscription pays about $19.80 monthly, so it takes roughly 17 months to beat a $300 one-off. Recurring wins in the long run and compounds in year two without new traffic — but check duration and churn before assuming it is the better deal.

How long do recurring commissions last?

It varies, and this is the most important term to check. Some pay for a fixed 12 or 24 months, some for the full lifetime of the customer's subscription, and some reduce the rate after an initial period. Lifetime deals are worth 3–5× a 12-month deal at the same headline percentage.

Do programs claw back commissions on cancellation?

Most simply stop paying when a customer cancels, which is expected. Some also reverse the initial commission if a refund or cancellation occurs within a short window, typically 30–60 days. That is why churn matters as much as rate: 40% of a leaky product can pay less than 20% of a sticky one.

Which recurring program should I start with?

Web hosting and website builders — easiest significant recurring programs to be approved for, with strong small-business retention. Email marketing converts well for creator audiences. B2B software pays the most per customer and is the hardest to get into, so treat it as the tier you climb toward.

How much traffic for $1,000 a month?

With $85 first-year value per conversion, a 3.5% click-through rate and a 2% conversion rate, roughly 17,000 monthly visitors produces about $1,000/month in year one. The difference from retail is what happens next: that figure grows in year two without any additional traffic, because existing customers keep paying.
