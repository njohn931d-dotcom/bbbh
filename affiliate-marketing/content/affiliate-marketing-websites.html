Why example sites earn so differently

When people look for affiliate marketing website examples, they usually want to copy a winning site. That instinct is backwards — you cannot out-compete an established site on its own topic. What you can copy is its structure: how it organises pages, which keyword patterns it targets, and how it converts a reader into a click.

The reason the models below earn so differently comes down to one number: revenue per visitor.

Models 1–4: the content-first sites

01

The product review site

  • Beginner friendly
  • $60–$150/yr
  • $8–$35 per 1k
  • Skill: writing

A library of “best [product] for [use case]” and “[product] review” pages in one product category. The classic affiliate site model, and still the most accessible — it needs no camera, no coding and no audience.

Structure that works: a hub page targeting the category's main keyword, then a cluster of long-tail pages (“best espresso machine for small kitchens”) each linking back to the hub. Add a comparison table above the fold on every page, and one piece of original evidence you generated yourself.

What breaks it: reviewing products you have never touched. Without first-hand experience your page looks identical to a thousand others, and search engines increasingly treat it that way.

02

The head-to-head comparison site

  • Low competition
  • Highest CVR
  • Skill: analysis

Entirely built on “[Product A] vs [Product B]” pages. Volumes are small — often 300–3,000/month — but the reader is one decision away from buying and needs a tiebreaker. Conversion rates commonly run double a generic listicle.

Why it is under-served: writing a fair comparison requires understanding both products' pricing models, limits and ideal use cases. That is real work, which is exactly why the competition is thin.

03

The software alternatives site

  • Repeatable
  • Very high intent
  • Skill: writing

Every popular product has a queue of people wanting to leave it. “[Popular brand] alternatives” searchers have already decided to switch — the most valuable position a buyer can be in — and big brands will never write these pages themselves.

Monetisation tip: many SaaS programs run 60–90 day cookies, so a reader who switches six weeks after reading still pays you. This model pairs naturally with recurring commissions.

04

The free-tool site

  • Build once
  • Best link magnet
  • Skill: light coding

A calculator, template generator, comparison picker or estimator that answers a question, then recommends the tool that solves the next step. Free tools earn backlinks — the hardest currency in SEO — because people link to utilities the way they link to statistics.

Why it out-converts: visitors stay on the page and interact, then receive a recommendation at the exact moment they have a result in front of them. This site's own affiliate income calculator is the pattern in miniature.

Models 5–8: the volume and structure plays

05

The deals and coupon site

  • High maintenance
  • Highest raw traffic
  • $4–$20 per 1k

“[Brand] discount code” searches spike enormously and convert at the checkout. The economics are brutal — low commission rates and expired codes destroy trust fast — but traffic volumes are enormous and seasonal peaks are dramatic.

Make it durable: pair every deal page with evergreen buying guides so the site does not collapse in February, and never publish a code you have not verified. Honesty about an expired code is what brings people back.

06

The tutorial and how-to site

  • Beginner friendly
  • High volume
  • Skill: teaching

Informational traffic converted later. Someone searches “how to set up a home office network”, learns from your guide, and clicks your recommended router or service. Volumes are much larger than commercial keywords and the competition is often weaker.

The technique: solve the problem completely and free first, then place tool recommendations exactly where the reader hits the step that requires buying something. Recommendations that arrive mid-task convert far better than a list bolted onto the end.

07

The niche news and blog site

  • Recurring visitors
  • Needs cadence
  • Skill: writing + sourcing

Covers a hobby or industry continuously, building a returning audience rather than relying only on search. The affiliate revenue is lower per visitor, but a loyal readership converts consistently and monetises multiple ways: affiliate, sponsorship, display ads and your own product.

Best fit for: niches with genuine ongoing news — cycling, drones, indie games, home brewing, local real estate. Requires the discipline to publish weekly forever.

08

The programmatic directory site

  • Advanced
  • Huge ceiling
  • Skill: data + build

Hundreds or thousands of pages generated from structured data — “best [service] in [city]”, “[product] under $500 with [feature]”, “compare [A] vs [B] vs [C]”. This is how large affiliate publishers scale past the reach of manual writing.

The rule that keeps this legal and ranking

If a page could be written without your data, do not publish it. Thin programmatic pages are precisely what spam policies target, and a few thousand of them can suppress an entire domain. Programmatic is a scaling layer on top of a quality site, never a shortcut to one.

Models 9–12: the audience and high-ticket plays

09

The newsletter-first site

  • Highest ROI
  • Owned audience
  • Skill: curation

The site exists mainly to capture emails; revenue comes from recommendations inside the emails. A 2,000-subscriber list that trusts you can out-earn 100,000 monthly pageviews, because a subscriber reading an email is already qualified.

Why it is the most durable model: you own the distribution. An algorithm update cannot take your list away. Every other model on this page becomes dramatically more valuable with a newsletter layered on top.

10

The YouTube-first site

  • Needs on-camera
  • Dual income
  • Highest trust

Video reviews convert better than written ones because viewers watch the product working. A channel focused on one category builds a compounding library where years-old videos keep earning, and monetises twice: affiliate links plus ad revenue.

The multiplier: publish a written version of every video on a companion site. The transcript ranks for the exact questions your viewers ask, and it is content you have already researched and scripted.

11

The personal finance comparison site

  • Compliance heavy
  • Very high payouts
  • Skill: research + rigour

Credit cards, insurance, mortgages, investing platforms and banking. These programs pay per approved application rather than per purchase, so you monetise the majority of visitors who never buy anything — $20–$500 per lead is normal.

Why competition is thin: financial promotions are regulated in most jurisdictions. You must be accurate, include required disclaimers, and never give regulated advice without a licence. Most publishers avoid the work, which is precisely why the payouts stay high.

12

The B2B review and lead-gen site

  • Credibility required
  • $500–$5,000+ per deal
  • Lowest competition

Deeply researched reviews, teardowns and buying criteria for business software and services. A single referred enterprise customer can be worth four or five figures in commission, so the site needs only a few thousand targeted visitors per month to be a real business.

How to enter it: pick one vertical you genuinely understand, publish procurement-grade content (implementation pitfalls, total cost of ownership, integration limits), then apply to vendor partner programs citing your audience and traffic. Expect an application and sometimes an interview. See high ticket affiliate marketing for the full playbook.

Which model fits your skills

If you write well

Product review, comparison, alternatives, tutorial. The largest pool of opportunity and the cheapest to start. Your moat is first-hand testing, not word count.

If you are comfortable on camera

YouTube-first, with a companion written site. Slower to start but the highest trust per visitor and two revenue streams from one piece of work.

If you can code

Free-tool sites and programmatic directories. Fewer competitors, better links, and the tools themselves become the marketing asset.

If you have domain expertise

B2B review and lead-gen, or finance. The highest payouts in affiliate marketing, and the least crowded, precisely because most publishers cannot clear the credibility bar.

What every successful affiliate site has in common

  1. A narrow focus. One category, deeply covered. Authority is far easier to build in a small pond.
  2. A commercial keyword map. Pages built from “best”, “vs”, “alternatives” and “[brand] review” patterns — not from whatever seems interesting to write.
  3. Original evidence. Screenshots, measured results, verified prices, photos of the actual product. This is the only thing that cannot be replicated by a competitor or summarised away by an AI answer.
  4. An email list. Every model above gets safer and more profitable with one behind it.
  5. Two or more payout structures. A retail program for conversion, a recurring or high-ticket program for margin.
  6. Visible disclosure and authorship. Required legally in most jurisdictions, and it materially improves conversion because it signals honesty.
  7. A maintenance habit. Quarterly refreshes of the top ten pages is usually the highest-ROI hour a publisher spends all quarter.

How to build yours without wasting a year

  1. Pick the model from your skills, not from this list

    Write down what you already have: writing ability, camera comfort, coding, subject expertise, or an existing audience. Choose the model that asset supports. Then run the revenue-per-visitor maths in the calculator to confirm a realistic traffic level pays a meaningful amount.

  2. Map 30 commercial keywords before writing anything

    Thirty buyer-intent keywords is roughly six months of content. Listing them first prevents the classic failure of publishing twenty pages and then realising you cannot think of a twenty-first.

  3. Publish the money pages first

    Go straight to “best X for Y” and “X vs Y”. Informational content can wait — it is slower to convert and you need early revenue signals to know your model is working.

  4. Add email capture by page ten

    Build the lead magnet once and place it on your two best-performing pages. Every subsequent model decision gets easier when you have an owned channel.

  5. Review at month six, not month two

    Judge the site on whether pages are indexing and getting impressions, not on revenue. Then double down on whichever pages are closest to page one and prune the ones going nowhere.

FAQ

What is the most profitable type of affiliate website?

B2B software review and alternatives sites, by a wide margin — a single referred enterprise customer can be worth hundreds or thousands in commission. Deals sites get the most raw traffic; free-tool sites earn the best links. Ranked by revenue per visitor: B2B software, finance lead-gen, recurring SaaS, courses, then retail.

How much traffic does an affiliate website need to make money?

A retail review site needs roughly 30,000 monthly visitors for about $1,000/month. A B2B or high-ticket site can hit the same revenue with 3,000–5,000, because the average commission is 10–50× larger. Traffic matters far less than what you are recommending. Model it here.

How much does it cost to build one?

$60–$150/year for a domain and hosting, plus $0–$30/month for an email tool once you capture leads. You can start free on a hosted platform but you will not own the asset. Skip paid themes, plugins and courses until the site earns — and never pay a fee to join an affiliate program.

How long until it makes money?

Three to six months for a first commission, twelve to eighteen months for meaningful monthly income in competitive niches, assuming consistent publishing. Low-competition verticals with high-ticket commissions move faster. Buying an existing site skips the wait, at a typical 30–40× monthly profit multiple.

Do affiliate websites still work in 2026?

Yes, but the thin-listicle model is dead. Search engines reward first-hand experience and original data, and AI summaries absorb generic queries. Sites that publish real testing, verified pricing and clear opinions outperform sites that aggregate other people's reviews.

Can I run one without showing my face?

Yes. Written review and comparison sites need no on-camera presence, and many successful publishers operate under a brand identity. You still need a credible author biography and editorial policy for trust — it just does not have to be a personal brand.

Is buying an affiliate website better than building one?

Buying compresses years of compounding into one transaction at a typical 30–40× monthly profit. Faster and more certain, but it needs capital and real diligence: check traffic concentration, commission concentration, and whether rankings depend on a single page or a single program.
