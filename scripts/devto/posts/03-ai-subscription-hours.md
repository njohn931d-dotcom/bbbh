Every few weeks a thread appears about whether some $20/month AI tool is worth it, and the argument usually runs on vibes: "it's only twenty bucks" versus "twenty bucks forever is two hundred and forty a year." Both positions are dodging the only question that matters, which is: **how many hours of your work does it cost, and how many does it save?**

Dollars are abstract. Hours are the thing you actually spend.

## The conversion

Take your realistic billable or effective hourly rate — the one from the last post, not the payroll number.

| Monthly price | Per year | Hours at $25/hr | Hours at $60/hr | Hours at $120/hr |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $10 | $120 | 4.8 h | 2.0 h | 1.0 h |
| $20 | $240 | 9.6 h | 4.0 h | 2.0 h |
| $30 | $360 | 14.4 h | 6.0 h | 3.0 h |
| $200 (team of 10) | $2,400 | 96 h | 40 h | 20 h |

At $60/hour, a $20 tool costs **four working hours a year**. That reframes the conversation completely: this is not a budgeting decision, it is a rounding error. Four hours a year to argue about it in Slack is already a net loss.

## The decision rule I use

> Does the tool save more hours than it costs in the same period?

One concrete test: does it save 20 minutes a week? That is roughly 17 hours a year. Against a 4-hour cost, that is a 4x return, and the answer is yes — stop reading, buy it.

If it saves 5 minutes a week, that is about 4 hours a year: break-even. Now it is a judgment call about whether you enjoy using it, and that is a legitimate reason to keep a subscription.

If you cannot name what it saves at all, you are not paying for a tool, you are paying for the intention of using it. That is the expensive category, and it does not care whether the price is $20 or $200.

## Where team pricing gets real

The reason to run the math is not the $20 seat. It is the team plan:

- 10 seats × $25/month = **$3,000/year**
- At a $60/hour blended rate: **50 hours of work**

Fifty hours is enough to justify an actual evaluation — a real comparison, not a poll. And note the asymmetry: the personal subscription should be decided in 60 seconds, the team plan deserves half a day. Most teams spend the time in exactly the reverse order.

## Three ways this accounting lies to you

1. **Learning time is not free.** If a tool takes 8 hours to learn and saves 4 hours a year, it never pays back. Be honest about adoption cost.
2. **Sunk-cost renewal.** Resubscribing is a fresh decision. The default is not "keep", it is "decide again".
3. **Counting the same saved hour twice.** If the tool saves you 10 minutes and you spend those 10 minutes in a different tool, you have not saved an hour.
